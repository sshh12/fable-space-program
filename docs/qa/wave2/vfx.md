# vfx: merged visual QA work list (wave 2)

**Verdict.** The package is not ready: the photo critic scored it 3.5 over 171 screenshots and the artifact critic 3.5 over 169, and no scene passes either lens. Three failures run through everything. The camera's exposure follows the effects, so a noon lift-off is rendered as dusk and the frame darkens by more than a stop when a splash, a fireball, an ignition flash or a canopy enters it. At night the plume lights nothing a player can see, so a lift-off is a black frame with a pastel jet. And most effects read as the shapes they are built from: a glow ball at separation, a flat disc at ignition, a white lens and a pinwheel for the reentry shock layer, a lampshade for the splash crown, paper wedges for solid motors, a matte ochre ball for an explosion, a closed bulb with a scale pattern for a vacuum plume. The critics' 39 findings merge into 28 items: 8 blockers, 13 major, 7 minor. I opened every cited screenshot (at full size, or as a labelled thumbnail plus zoomed crops where the defect is a detail) and re-measured the main figures. Every defect is there, so nothing was dropped. Five statements by the critics are corrected or qualified from the pictures and the lab code, each in its item: the size of the separation glow (item 3), the capsule's stillness in the splash (item 6), the fireball's brightness against the sky (item 8), the size of the vacuum bulbs (item 10) and the timing of the cut-away picture (item 17).

Items are ordered by how much a player would notice. Where the critics rated an item differently, the higher severity is kept and both ratings are shown. Three items (20, 25, 26) are sub-defects that the artifact critic reported inside a larger finding and that have a cause of their own; I split them out and rated them myself, and each says so. Screenshot paths are relative to `out/screens/vfx/`; code paths are relative to the repo root. "Hand-off" means `docs/handoff/vfx.md`, "the brief" means `PROMPT.txt`, and "the matrix" means the pictures written by `tests/browser/vfx/matrix.spec.ts`. Pixel values are 8-bit sRGB medians of a patch unless a line says otherwise.

State of the pictures: the matrix was re-run on 2026-11-11 (33 of 33 specs passed), so every matrix picture is current. The eight `perf-*.png` pictures date from 2026-11-10 22:46-22:51, before the last commit to the package (ee55fc4, 23:09, which changed `plumes.json`, `feature.ts` and `plumes.ts`), and the `dev/` pictures are working shots from 10 November. Where an item rests on one of those, it says so. The matrix is shot in deterministic mode, which freezes the blue-noise offset; item 24 covers what that does to the pictures.

The table at the end shows which items the package can fix alone and which need another owner.

## 1. The exposure follows the effects: a noon lift-off is rendered as dusk, and the frame pumps at every bright event

- **Severity:** blocker (artifact: blocker; photo: major, for the splash sequence only)
- **Screenshots:** `liftoff/t0-day.png`, `liftoff/t3-day.png`, `liftoff/t10-day.png`, `liftoff-chase/t0-day.png`, `liftoff-chase/t3-day.png`, `liftoff-chase/t6-day.png`, `liftoff-chase/t10-day.png`, `engine-atlas-9m/sl-full.png` against `engine-lyra-d/sl-min.png`, `ascent/2km.png` against `ascent/30km.png`, `splash/before.png`, `splash/impact-0.1s.png`, `splash/impact-0.4s.png`, `splash/impact-1s.png`, `splash/impact-2.5s.png`, `explosion/day-0.03s.png` against `explosion/day-3.5s.png`, `vacuum-ignition/ignition-0.3s.png`, `staging/2.56s-separation.png`
- **Checked:** visible in all of them. Re-measured:
  - Large plume by day: sky (33,44,62) and ground (61,53,42) in `engine-atlas-9m/sl-full.png`, against sky (90,105,128) and ground (126,116,102) in `engine-lyra-d/sl-min.png`, which is the same lab scene with a small engine at minimum throttle. All seven pad pictures have the same navy sky (33-35, 44-46, 63-64).
  - Pixels at 250 or more in all channels: 0 in `engine-atlas-9m/sl-full.png`, 28 in `liftoff/t10-day.png`.
  - Splash: the sky's red channel goes 208, 135, 104, 89, 117, 144, 161 at before, +0.1, +0.4, +1, +1.8, +2.5 and +5 s, and the sea's green channel 122 to 18 at +1 s, while white water covers about 1 % of the frame (0.7 % to 1.4 % by a threshold count). The brightest crown pixel is 202 to 213, so the crown is grey.
  - Explosion: sky 71 at 0.03 s to 113 at 3.5 s.
  - Ignition and separation: lab ground 90 at 0.05 s, 60 at 0.3 s, 111 at 4 s after ignition; 105, 85, 105 around separation.
  - Three more signs the critics did not list. The sea-level solid-motor tiles have the same dark sky (33,44,62) although no flame is visible in them (item 7). `splash/cut-away-6s.png` is dark again (sky 99) with two canopies in view and white water on 0.2 % of the frame, and every picture on the chutes sheet has a sky of 44 to 75 (red channel, top of the frame). The read-out in `dev/f9-int-reentry-side.png` (integration lab, 10 November) says EV100 16.50, which is the meter's upper clamp `EV_MAX`, over a sunlit Earth.
- **Defect:** Every daylight view with a large plume is rendered about 2.5 to 3 stops under-exposed: a noon lift-off reads as dusk with a navy sky. Because the meter holds the flame under white, the 1e6 cd/m^2 core barely clips and has no bloom, so the jets look pastel (item 9). The same mechanism makes the frame pump: the whole scene darkens by well over a stop when the splash appears and brightens again as it subsides, the sky brightens over 3.5 s as a fireball cools, and the planet behind the stage darkens for the frames of the ignition flash.
- **Real reference:** Daylight launch footage (Saturn V, Falcon 9, SLS): sky, pad and terrain stay at normal daylight exposure; the flame is a clipped white-yellow core with glare around it and the steam cloud is brilliant white. In recovery footage the exposure does not react to the splash: the white water simply clips. A fireball does not darken the sky around it.
- **Likely cause:** The core exposure meter, not the plume data. `src/render/core/post/exposureMeter.ts` (lines 23-29) builds its histogram from the final HDR target at every pixel that has geometry depth, that is after the vfx transparents are composited. `src/render/core/post/exposure.ts` line 203 then raises the EV until the 99th percentile of lit pixels is below `HIGHLIGHT_CAP` (4 x mid-grey), and line 204 gives the histogram full weight from a lit fraction of 2 % (`LIT_FULL_BRIGHT`, the rule from the resolution of `docs/requests/vfx-3.md`). So a plume, plume-lit steam, a white crown or a sunlit canopy on about 1 % of the lit pixels sets the exposure. The cap in `src/render/vfx/feature.ts` (`DAYLIGHT_WHITE = 12000`, lines 80 and 175) limits only the hint for emitters seen against the sky and does not touch this path, so hand-off section 5.12 ("by day a plume leaves the exposure where the sun puts it and clips") does not hold in the pictures. My reading of the splash numbers: in `splash/before.png` the meter exposes for the dark sea alone (sky near white), and the 99th-percentile term takes over once the white water appears, so both ends of the swing come from the meter. Part of the first step (before to +0.1 s) may also be the lab's initial adaptation; the swing between +0.4 s, +1 s and +2.5 s is not.
- **Fix direction:** File a request to core-support: (a) meter the HDR target before `drawTransparent`, or accept an exclusion for emissive transparents; (b) while the sun is up, bound highlight protection to the sunny-16 prior plus about 0.5 EV, so flames clip by 2 to 3 stops and bloom; (c) make the daylight target percentile-based so small highlights (white water, a canopy, a fireball) clip instead of driving the meter. Inside the package keep crown and foam at or below sunlit white. Then re-shoot the lift-off, ascent, staging, splash, chutes and explosion sheets. Add two specs: the sky luminance of the pad scene changes by less than 0.5 EV between engines off and engines on by day, and the sky's pixel value stays within +-5 % through the splash sequence.

## 2. A night lift-off is a black frame: nothing the plume lights is visible

- **Severity:** blocker (photo: blocker; artifact: major)
- **Screenshots:** `liftoff-chase/t0-night.png`, `liftoff-chase/t3-night.png`, `liftoff-chase/t6-night.png`, `liftoff-chase/t10-night.png`, `liftoff/t0-night.png`, `liftoff/t3-night.png`, `liftoff/t10-night.png`, `explosion/night-0.2s.png`, `explosion/night-1.5s.png`
- **Checked:** visible in all nine. Re-measured: 65 % to 97 % of each lift-off frame is at 4/255 or below (97 % in `liftoff/t0-night.png`, 65 % in `liftoff-chase/t6-night.png`). In `liftoff-chase/t0-night.png` the apron beside the mount is (2,1,1), the apron farther out (0,0,0) and the hull above the engines (1,0,0). The plume column at T+10 has the same values by night (228,215,220) as by day (229,216,221) in the same crop. The two explosion frames are 92 % and 98 % black: the ball is the only thing in them.
- **Defect:** A night lift-off is a black frame with a pastel jet and one patch of lit cloud beside it. The mount clips to white and the smoke is lit, but the pad surface, the surrounding ground and the vehicle body stay at 0 to 4 of 255, even with a 6 x gain applied to the picture; the vehicle is an invisible silhouette at its own launch. The camera sits at a daylight exposure for the flame, so nothing the flame lights shows. The night fireball likewise lights nothing beneath it and has no glow halo. The brief's "at night: ground and smoke lit by the plume" is not visibly met.
- **Real reference:** Night launches (Artemis I, Apollo 17, Falcon Heavy at night): the plume is a clipped white-yellow column with a large glare; pad, tower, ground and the whole cloud are lit orange-white for hundreds of metres to kilometres, the vehicle's flanks and aft glow, the sky brightens. An explosion at night lights the ground and its own smoke.
- **Likely cause:** Three things add up.
  1. Exposure. `src/render/vfx/feature.ts`: the plume's exposure hint is capped at `DAYLIGHT_WHITE = 12000` (hand-off 5.12: "an eighth of their luminance, at most 12,000 cd/m^2 ... at night the camera is stopped down for it"), which pins the night exposure at daylight level; the meter rule of item 1 then settles on the brightest plume-lit steam right at the jet, and the ground sits about 9 stops lower.
  2. Light geometry. `plumeLights()` (lines 199-224) puts one zero-radius capsule light per cluster on the jet axis, from one exit radius below the nozzles to 1.2 x the core length. From T+0 to about T+3 that segment ends below the pad plane, so for ground points the nearest point of the light is at or under the surface and N.L is near zero; the vertical hull walls above the nozzles see it end-on, at grazing incidence. There is no bounce from the lit cloud or ground.
  3. Data. `light.efficacy` 8.5 cd/N gives about 6e7 cd for the booster, roughly ten times less than what lights a pad like daylight (the photo critic quotes 6.49 cd/N as the value in effect; check which applies). `explosion.luminance` 70,000 and `lightEfficacy` 0.008 are low.
- **Fix direction:** Let the flame clip at night: cap the plume hint far lower when the scene is dark (for example min(L/8, 1000-2000 cd/m^2), or scale the cap by the sun's illuminance at the camera) so the meter settles on plume-lit ground and cloud and the jet blooms. End the capsule at the plume foot and add a second light where the jet meets the ground for the spreading flame and the lit steam (a bounce light per burning cluster: intensity = albedo x intercepted flux), or request a source radius on `LocalLight` from core-support so the capsule is an area light (same request as item 13). Raise the efficacy (aim for about 1e4 lux at 300 m from the booster). Make the fireball a sphere light of its own radius and raise `lightEfficacy`. Verify: the hull above the engines reaches mid-grey at T+3 and the apron 100 m out is clearly visible at T+10. Add a spec that the pad 50 to 150 m from the mount exceeds a luminance threshold at night with engines on.

## 3. Stage separation is a cream glow ball; the retro and ullage motors show no jets

- **Severity:** blocker (photo: blocker; artifact: major)
- **Screenshots:** `plumes-close/staging-separation-70m.png`, `staging/2.56s-separation.png`, `staging/2.4s-ullage.png`, `staging/2.9s-retro.png`, `staging/3.6s-coast.png`, `plumes-close/staging-retro-70m.png`
- **Checked:** visible in all six. In `plumes-close/staging-separation-70m.png` the glow is brighter than the sunlit hull and covers the interstage and both stage ends. It is larger than the photo critic's "one and a half stage diameters across": the visible glow measures about 330 x 400 px around a stage about 90 px thick, so it reaches one and a half to two stage diameters out from the hull on each side. In a 3 x crop of `staging/2.4s-ullage.png` the ullage motors are two soft cream fans with no core.
- **Defect:** Separation is a smooth cream glow ball about four stage diameters across with a soft cauliflower rim, brighter than the sunlit hull and hiding half the vehicle. No separate retro or ullage jets exist: the ullage motors are two featureless cream cones beside the interstage, and the retro motors are a diffuse blob with two clumps of identical dots (item 22). It reads as a halo sprite, which the brief rules out.
- **Real reference:** Saturn V first-stage separation film: eight retro jets as separate short white-yellow streaks with thin smoke, the interstage falling through a brief orange fireball. Falcon 9 and Titan staging: a brief flash and thin, fast, nearly transparent gas puffs gone in under half a second, a thin expanding translucent gas ring, a few tumbling pieces of very different sizes; the stages' edges stay crisp.
- **Likely cause:** `src/render/vfx/director.ts` case `'decoupled'` (line 353): 1400 x scale `gas` particles (`plumes.json` `particles.gas`: size 0.3-1.1 m growing at 0.35 x puffSpeed, opacity 0.2, life to 1.1 s) drawn as round Gaussian sprites (`particles.ts` fragment shader, `exp(-3.5 r^2)`, line 371), plus `staging.flashIntensity` 60000. The retro and ullage motors use `plume.solid` at vacuum pressure, where the core is hidden as in item 7.
- **Fix direction:** Cut the gas count and opacity by an order of magnitude and emit a thin radial sheet from the separation plane that expands at 200 m/s or more and is gone in 0.3-0.4 s (opacity falling as 1/r^2); keep the pyro flash as a light of a few frames. Make the retro and ullage jets the subject: give each motor a visible core jet (item 7).

## 4. Engine ignition in vacuum is a flat cream disc standing across the axis

- **Severity:** blocker (photo: blocker, in the same finding as item 3; artifact: major)
- **Screenshots:** `vacuum-ignition/ignition-0.05s.png`, `vacuum-ignition/ignition-0.3s.png`, `vacuum-ignition/ignition-1s.png`, `staging/4.6s-ignition.png`
- **Checked:** visible in all four. In `vacuum-ignition/ignition-0.3s.png` the ellipse is about 250 px tall (y 250-500) on a stage about 60 px thick, with a dithered left edge and a sharp right face at the engine plane; `vacuum-ignition/ignition-1s.png` still shows a cream residue. The ground darkens for these frames (item 1).
- **Defect:** Ignition of the vacuum stage, in both scenes, is a flat cream ellipse several stage diameters tall standing across the axis at the engine plane, with a sharp downstream face and a speckled edge. It is still there 0.3 s after ignition and leaves a cream residue at 1 s although `flashTime` is 0.25 s. Its colour and luminance are uniform. It reads as a lens or halo sprite stuck on the stage.
- **Real reference:** Upper-stage ignition in vacuum (J-2, Merlin Vacuum, RL10 footage): a brief, wide, translucent puff of unburnt propellant that expands in all directions and washes over the stage below, then a faint steady plume. Hydrolox: a few frames of orange-red flash, then an almost invisible blue plume.
- **Likely cause:** `src/render/vfx/plumes.ts` line 159 adds `flashColor * lens.w * k0 * inlet * front * exp(-xc/(8 re)) * exp(-sq(r/(1.3 rBulk + re)))`: an unmodulated Gaussian ball, 3.2 widths wide radially (line 111), cut along the axis by `inlet` and by the start-up `front` (line 114) at the distance the front has run. In the first tenths of a second that is a few metres, so the ball is sliced into a thin slab that is seen as a disc; six nozzles sum to one disc.
- **Fix direction:** Model the flash as an expanding shell of gas that fills forward and sideways (radius grows with frontSpeed x age in every direction away from the nozzle, soft on all sides) instead of a ball cut by a plane. Modulate it with the plume noise, let it travel with the gas, tint it by propellant, bring it to zero at `flashTime`, never opaque. In vacuum let it spread widely and thin out.

## 5. The reentry shock layer is a white lens from the side and a pinwheel from the front; the wake is a dusty tube and is missing from behind

- **Severity:** blocker (artifact: blocker; photo: major)
- **Screenshots:** `reentry-views/front-1.0.png`, `reentry-views/front-0.3.png`, `reentry-views/behind-1.0.png`, `reentry-views/behind-0.3.png`, `reentry-views/side-close-1.0.png`, `reentry/plasma-1.0-day.png`, `reentry/plasma-0.3-day.png`, `reentry/plasma-1.0-night.png`, `dev/f9-int-reentry-side.png` (integration lab, real capsule, 10 November)
- **Checked:** visible in all nine. The wake measures (130,103,111) at intensity 1.0 and (89,71,73) at 0.3: the same dusty rose at mid-grey. In `reentry/plasma-0.3-day.png` the wake ends about 250 px behind the capsule and a wider band begins above its axis further back. In both `behind` pictures the sparks sit in the upper right quadrant only and the veil covers the left third. A 4 x crop of the rim in `reentry-views/side-close-1.0.png` shows the lattice of about 2 px cells.
- **Defect:**
  - From the side (chase view, and the integration lab too): the shock layer is a flat, clipped white disc with a hard outline and no colour gradient, stuck on the heat shield like a lens.
  - From the front: a pinwheel. High-contrast radial petals converge on one pole at the stagnation point (a visible pinch), inside a hard-edged inner ellipse, surrounded by a ring of crystalline glitter cells, with a dark notch where the shoulder cuts the ring.
  - From behind: no wake at all. A flat mottled mauve disc inside a white glitter ring (a solar-eclipse look), hairline ruler-straight orange sparks in one quadrant, and a soft mauve veil over the left third of the frame.
  - The wake is a matte grey-pink translucent tube of straight streaks at mid-grey luminance, without glow, the same hue from head to tail and at intensity 0.3 and 1.0. At 0.3 it stops halfway and a wider trail band starts off its axis further back (a visible break and kink).
  - The capsule's own surfaces are not lit by the plasma.
- **Real reference:** Orion EFT-1 and Artemis I, Apollo, Soyuz and Dragon onboard footage: a smooth, streaming, flickering sheath of orange-pink to white-hot gas past the shoulder and filling the view aft, no fixed pattern on the shield face, streamers shed irregularly, bright flashes of ablator all round. From outside (Hayabusa, Stardust, ATV-1, Orion long-range tracking): a compact clipped white-yellow head with a glare much larger than the body, glow wrapping the shoulder, no ring and no spokes, an incandescent wake that tapers without a break into the trail.
- **Likely cause:** `src/render/vfx/plasma.ts` fragment shader.
  - Lens: the layer is a thin shell at the standoff distance (`front = exp(-sq((sd - 0.95 delta)/(0.3 delta)))`, `face` only on windward normals).
  - Pinwheel: lines 269-288 stretch the streak noise along `v = x - n * n.x`, which vanishes at the stagnation point (clamped at 0.05, line 274), so every streak converges on a pole; `streak = (0.25 + 2.6 n^2)(0.55 + 0.9 n2)` (line 285) is a 10:1 contrast.
  - Inner ellipse: `plasmaBody` (lines 195-209) switches between the spherical cap and the rim, which changes the wall normal abruptly.
  - Glitter ring: beyond the shoulder the noise lod (`t * fp * 1.5 / Rb`) is far below a half-resolution pixel and aliases into cells.
  - Wake colour: the wake is scaled by `0.16/Rb` (line 308) and mixed towards `hot`, a grey (luminance x (1, 0.96, 1.05), line 250), which desaturates it; `plumes.json` `reentry.colors` middle entry is [1.0, 0.5, 0.62] and one value colours the whole volume.
  - No wake from behind: the near-eye cut `smoothstep(1 Rb, 6 Rb, t)` (line 312) removes the wake for a camera 16 m behind; the veil is what remains (hand-off section 6).
  - Break at 0.3: `trail.ts` line 341 sets the trail's taper from `wakeLengthRadii x radius x (0.3 + 0.7 plasma)` while the wake volume fades with `exp(-xs/wakeLen)`, so at low intensity they do not overlap.
  - Unlit capsule: `reentry.light.efficacy` 0.015.
- **Fix direction:** Shield: add a soft post-shock glow envelope (0.3-0.5 Rb ahead of the shield, wrapping the shoulder, smooth falloff) on top of the thin front, let the stagnation region clip and bloom, and grade it from white at the stagnation region to orange-pink at the shoulder. Blend from isotropic, time-varying turbulence at the stagnation point to streaks only where |v| is large, and cut streak contrast on the face to about +-25 %. Use a rounded body distance so the normal is continuous across cap and shoulder. Band-limit the shoulder noise to the pixel footprint and end the layer with a soft falloff, not a ring. Emit sparks all round the rim. Wake: five to ten times the luminance near the body with a saturated ramp by temperature (white core, orange edge) that cools along its length, continuous into the trail (same axis, same radius, overlapping fades). Inside the wake draw a streaming sheath instead of cutting everything within 6 Rb, and fade symmetrically around the eye so no one-sided veil is left. Raise the local light so the back shell takes an orange rim.

## 6. The splash is a white disc, then a lampshade, then torn paper; droplets are grey confetti; the foam is a speckled decal

- **Severity:** blocker (both lenses)
- **Screenshots:** `splash/impact-0.1s.png`, `splash/impact-0.4s.png`, `splash/impact-1s.png`, `splash/impact-1.8s.png`, `splash/impact-2.5s.png`, `splash/impact-5s.png`, `splash/close-0.4s.png`, `splash/close-1s.png`
- **Checked:** visible in all eight. A 4 x crop of the foam in `splash/impact-5s.png` shows the hard elliptical outline, the lace-like speckle and single dark-blue pixels inside the white. `splash/close-1s.png` shows the capsule through blotchy holes and soft round mist discs above and to the right of the crown. One correction: the photo critic's "the capsule stands still and upright" is the lab script (`labs/vfx/scenes.ts` lines 594-599 give it a 0.9 m damped heave and no tilt), not something the package draws.
- **Defect:**
  - +0.1 s: a pure white flat ellipse with a fur of short, straight radial needles.
  - +0.4 s: the crown is a smooth-walled, opaque, evenly light-grey truncated cone (a bucket or lampshade) with a clean elliptical foot and a paper-cut rim with a few sawtooth notches; the capsule is hidden inside. It reads as a solid mesh.
  - +1 s: torn-paper fingers; close up, blurred low-resolution blobs with blotchy holes, plus round soft mist-ball discs beside them.
  - Droplets: thousands of identical matte grey dashes of one size in an even annulus (confetti), darker than the crown, without sparkle, still hanging as a flat disc at +1.8 s.
  - Afterwards: a speckled white ring decal with a perfect elliptical outline and a hard outer edge. The water itself never changes.
- **Real reference:** Apollo, Orion (EFT-1, Artemis I) and Crew Dragon splashdowns: an irregular bright wall of white water thrown up and outward one to two capsule heights, asymmetric because the capsule arrives with horizontal speed, lumpy, translucent at the ligaments, breaking at once into ragged curtains and chunky spray of very different sizes that drifts downwind with bright glints on the drops; a rebound and collapse in the middle; then a boiling patch of pale turquoise aerated water with foam streaks and ring waves spreading for tens of metres while the capsule heaves and rocks.
- **Likely cause:** `src/render/vfx/splash.ts`. Crown (`CROWN_WGSL`): an axisymmetric thin sheet about the vertical with analytic rise and lean and a Gaussian cross-section at `opticalDepth` 3.2 (opaque), marched at half resolution (`crown.steps` 30); rim and tear noise only in angle and at low frequency (`ang*0.55`, 1.9, 2.6, 7.0), tearing only near the rim (line 181); lit almost uniformly (line 188: sun x (0.38 + 0.42 facing) plus ambient), so the wall has no shading. Foam (`FOAM_WGSL`): a hard `rho < 1` ellipse from one analytic radius (line 312) with a smoothstep edge (line 89); ring and mottle saturate to white at age 0; thresholded noise gives the speckle and pinholes. Droplets: `particles.ts` `KIND_DROPLET` is lit diffusely with its own, dimmer lighting (glints exist only for ice and chips); 8-30 mm drops are widened to a pixel and dimmed (lines 349-350); 16000 from three ring emitters with an even angular spread; `mistCount` 160.
- **Fix direction:** Break the symmetry: per-azimuth launch speed, height and tear time from low-frequency noise, biased by the entry velocity. Build the crown from a noisy thickness field (lumps in angle and height, two or three octaves) with optical depth from about 0.3 in ligaments to over 3 at the root, forward-scatter highlights and self-shadow, and hand its upper half to particles of widely varying size earlier. Give it a real body of mist (a volume that lingers and drifts, not 160 particles). Droplets: a wide size distribution with a few large blobs, sun glints, clustered into fingers, lit with the crown's radiometry. Foam: outline perturbed by +-40 % with two or three noise octaves and drift streaks, a soft edge, a turquoise tint under the white, no solid white disc at age 0, the threshold filtered so no pinholes remain. Ask the ocean package for a ring-wave disturbance at the entry point.

## 7. Solid motors in air are flat sand-coloured wedges with no flame

- **Severity:** blocker (photo: blocker; artifact: major)
- **Screenshots:** `engine-solids/sl-les.png`, `engine-solids/sl-retro.png`, `engine-solids/10km-les.png`, `engine-solids/10km-retro.png`, `engine-solids/30km-les.png` (sheet: `engine-solids.png`)
- **Checked:** visible in all five. At sea level and 10 km each motor is a beige wedge with ruler-straight edges and a pencil-point tip, about as bright as the ground behind it, with stipple and no bright root; `engine-solids/30km-les.png` is a white comet tip in a dim fan with a bright curved outline.
- **Defect:** Solid motors in air (escape motor, retro motors) are flat sand-coloured wedges with ruler-straight edges and pencil-point tips, uniformly stippled: no flame, no incandescent core, no glare anywhere in a 190 m frame, no billowing. Four of them read as paper cones or searchlight beams. From 30 km up the same motors flip to a white comet tip in a dim fan with a bright parabolic outline, so the two regimes look like two unrelated effects.
