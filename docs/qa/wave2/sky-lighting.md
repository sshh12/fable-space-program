# sky-lighting: merged visual QA work list (wave 2)

**Verdict.** The package is not ready: the photo critic scored it 4.5 and the artifact critic 5, each over the same 28 screenshots, and both name the same failures. With the sun in frame the picture is a stock lens-flare effect (14 hairlines to the frame edges, a grey glow ball with a rim, four bright pastel discs). At the darkest exposure the game can reach, the night sky is an almost black frame. A distant body blinks dark and changes colour and shape where its impostor hands over to geometry. What holds up is the data and the photometry: the star fields and the Milky Way at a hand-set EV -5 look good, and no lattice or cube seam shows. The critics' 21 findings merge into 13 items: 1 blocker, 5 major, 7 minor. I opened every cited screenshot and re-measured the main figures; every defect is there, so nothing was dropped. Item 9 (star shimmer) is the one defect a still cannot show; it rests on a spec log that I did not re-run.

Items are ordered by how much a player would notice. Where the critics rated an item differently, the higher severity is kept and both ratings are shown. Paths are relative to the repo root. "Hand-off" means `docs/handoff/sky-lighting.md`. The table at the end shows which items the package can fix alone and which need another owner.

---

## 1. The starburst is 14 constant-width hairlines that run to the frame edges

- **Severity:** blocker (photo: blocker, artifact: major)
- **Screenshots:** `out/screens/sky/sun-at-earth-limb-leo-flare.png`, `out/screens/sky/flare-deep-space-ev12.png`, `out/screens/sky/flare-deep-space-ev9.png`, `out/screens/sky/flare-off-axis-35deg-ev12.png`, `out/screens/sky/flare-sun-just-outside-frame-ev12.png`, `out/screens/sky/perf-1440p-leo-horizon.png`, `out/screens/integration/exposure-sun-settled.png`
- **Checked:** visible in all seven. Re-measured on `flare-deep-space-ev12.png`: 14 spikes 25.7 deg apart; the vertical spike has the same 3 px footprint at 60, 150 and 300 px from the sun (peak 230 over a glow of 159, 144 over 49, 77 over 0).
- **Defect:** The diffraction starburst is 14 identical, ruler-straight lines at exact 25.7 deg spacing, with no taper and no softening. Beyond the first quarter of its length a spike is a 2 px line of constant width, still 27/255 at 700 px (33 deg) from the sun. The lines cross the night-side Earth and the ghosts equally sharp, cross the whole 97 deg frame in the off-axis view, and still fan across a black picture when the sun is just outside the frame. At the settled automatic exposure in the integration lab they are fainter but still there. It reads at a glance as a vector asterisk drawn over the image.
- **Real reference:** In ISS, Shuttle and Apollo photographs the rays of a sunstar from a 7-blade iris are wedges: as wide as the burned-out disc at the base, tapering to nothing within a few degrees (2 to 6 diameters of the blown-out core, rarely more than 5-10 deg even at f/16-22, absent at wide apertures), soft-edged, unequal in length and brightness because the blades are not identical, with a slight colour smear along the ray. A sun just outside the frame gives haze at that edge, not lines across the picture.
- **Likely cause:** `src/render/sky/flare.ts`: `STARBURST.spikeWidth = 0.0009` rad with `spikeProfile()`, a Gaussian of constant angular width across the spike (about 0.6 px at 720p and 60 deg, so only temporal anti-aliasing holds the line together) times `1 / (1 + theta^2 / L^2)` along it, with no end and no per-spike variation. That width is five times narrower than the sun's angular radius (0.0047 rad): it is the diffraction pattern of a point source, not of a 0.53 deg disc. The spikes get 45 % of the starburst energy (`1 - FLARE_HALO_SHARE`), 0.18 % of the sun's illuminance, so the line stays above the tonemap's black for hundreds of pixels. Shader: `FLARE_WGSL` in `src/render/sky/shaders.ts` (`gw`, `along`, `spikes`).
- **Fix direction:** Convolve the spike set with the solar disc: make the across-profile half width the sun's angular radius (`frame.sunDir.w`; the chord profile of a disc, or a Gaussian of about half the solar radius) in quadrature with the lens term, and let the width grow with theta so the peak falls at least as theta^-3 and each ray dissolves into the glare as a fan. Give each of the 7 blade pairs its own seeded amplitude and length (plus or minus 20-30 %) and a small angular irregularity, keep the wavelength smear, and cut the spikes' share to 10-15 % of the starburst. Re-derive `spikeIntegral` and `flareRadiance` (the CPU model). Add a display-image check: spike width at 60 px from the sun at least the disc diameter, no spike pixel above 3/255 beyond about 8 deg at EV 12-15, nothing but haze when the sun is off screen. Re-take the flare views at the metered exposure (EV 14-15) as well as at EV 9-12.

## 2. The glare around the sun is a grey ball with a visible rim

- **Severity:** major (photo: major, artifact: major)
- **Screenshots:** `out/screens/sky/sun-at-earth-limb-leo-flare.png`, `out/screens/sky/perf-1440p-leo-horizon.png`, `out/screens/sky/flare-deep-space-ev12.png`, `out/screens/integration/exposure-sun-settled.png`
- **Checked:** visible in all four, plainest in the 1440p view and the integration view. Re-measured radial profile in the limb view (median 8-bit luma over the upper half): 251 at 20 px, 208 at 40, 129 at 80, 81 at 120, 44 at 160, 26 at 180, 12 at 200, 3 at 220, 1 at 240, 0 from 260. The tail is (12, 12, 10) at 200 px: blue falls first.
- **Defect:** The veiling glare is a neutral grey cone that ends at a definite radius, about 0.32 rad. The profile is a near-linear ramp that is still falling by 15 levels per 20 px when it hits black, so the eye traces a circle around it, with a khaki cast in the last 40 px. Between 200 and 260 px the HDR glare falls only 2.2 times while the display falls from 12/255 to 0. It reads as a glow sprite pasted over the frame and over the dark Earth below the limb, and the requirement names "halo sprites" as excluded.
- **Real reference:** Glare around the sun in orbital photography (ISS, Apollo Hasselblad) has no outer edge: a burned-out core, then a tail that keeps thinning and merges into a haze that usually reaches the frame edges. Blacks near the sun are lifted, not zero. The glow is white to slightly warm with no colour cast at its end, and no circle can be traced around it.
- **Likely cause:** `src/render/sky/flare.ts`: a single power law for the halo (`HALO_POWER = 1.5`, a pure theta^-3 law; `STARBURST.haloCore` 0.012 rad; `FLARE_HALO_SHARE` 0.55 of `STARBURST.energy` 0.004), evaluated in `FLARE_WGSL` (`halo = t.shape.y * pow(1.0 + x * x, -FLARE_HALO_POWER)`). The rim itself is made by the display transform: `src/render/core/post/tonemap.ts` (AgX plus the "mild punchy look") gives about 30 display levels per stop down to a hard zero 12.5 stops under mid-grey and crushes about 3.5 stops above black into about 1 stop, so a theta^-3 tail loses its last 44 levels in 55 px. The core's bloom (4 %, linear) adds too little to hide it. The hand-off (sections 3 and 7) records the finite radius as a property of the tonemap and leaves it.
- **Fix direction:** Two parts. Sky: replace the single theta^-3 halo by a tight core plus a wide, weak skirt falling as theta^-2 (the CIE veiling-glare law) and a near-uniform veiling term (stray light, a fraction of a percent of the in-frame source spread over the field), renormalised to the same total energy, so that at the metered exposure of a sunlit scene the glare is still above 1/255 at the frame edge. Core: file a request to core-support for an asymptotic black toe that rolls off over at least 3 stops with no added contrast below about 4 stops under mid-grey; the same toe hides the Milky Way (items 3 and 7). Add a spec on the tonemapped image: radial profile monotonic; from the first radius where it is below 16/255 it takes at least a doubling of the radius to reach 0 (slope under about 0.2 levels per px where it meets its floor); R = G = B within 3 % along the tail.

## 3. The night sky is almost black at the darkest exposure the game can reach

- **Severity:** major (photo: major, artifact: major)
- **Screenshots:** `out/screens/sky/leo-night-dark-adapted.png`, `out/screens/sky/pbr-spheres-leo-night.png`, `out/screens/sky/milky-way-band-ev-3.png`; for comparison `out/screens/sky/starfield-deep-space-ev-5.png`, `out/screens/sky/crux-centaurus-ev-5.png`
- **Checked:** `leo-night-dark-adapted.png` is a black frame with a few dozen dots. Re-measured: 99.2 % of pixels at 2/255 or below, 0.23 % above 8/255, 47 stars peaking at 64/255 or more and 168 at 32/255 or more. The Large Magellanic Cloud appears only after multiplying the image by six.
- **Defect:** At the exposure the game actually reaches in darkness (the auto-exposure floor, EV100 -2.9) the night sky from orbit is nearly empty. The Milky Way is at 1-5/255 (the hand-off's own figures: 2.1 at the galactic centre, 1.0 in Cygnus) and the Large Magellanic Cloud at (3.2, 1.9, 4.3)/255, which no display shows. The good galactic-centre, Crux and Orion views are taken at a hand-set EV -5 that play cannot reach: `EV_MIN` is -3 and clamps the player's exposure compensation as well.
- **Real reference:** From the night side of an orbit or from cislunar space the Milky Way is the main feature of the sky. ISS night photography (ISO 3200-12800, f/1.4-2.8, 0.5-2 s, about EV100 -4 to -7) shows a bright structured Milky Way and thousands of stars; Apollo crews in the Moon's shadow described a sky crowded with stars.
- **Likely cause:** Not the sky's photometry (about 5e-4 cd/m^2 for the band is right) but where it lands. `src/render/core/post/exposure.ts`: `EV_DARK_FLOOR = EV_MIN = -3` (comment: "stars and the Milky Way visible") puts 5e-4 to 1e-3 cd/m^2 about 4.7 to 5.6 stops under mid-grey, in the toe of `tonemap.ts`, which crushes that range to 1-5/255. Both files are core-owned, and the brief forbids a brightness knob in the sky, so the sky cannot fix it alone. The sky criterion ("faintly visible") was passed at 2/255.
- **Fix direction:** File a request to core-support: let the dark floor and `EV_MIN` fall to about EV -6, reached by slow dark adaptation (tens of seconds, as the eye) and only while nothing lit and no emitter is metered; and soften the toe (same request as items 2 and 7). Then re-shoot `leo-night-dark-adapted` and `milky-way-band` at the settled automatic exposure and assert display levels there in `tests/browser/sky/stars.spec.ts`: Milky Way mean at least 12/255 in Sagittarius and Crux (target about 25/255 at the galactic centre and 40/255 in the Crux region), and well over a thousand stars above 16/255 in a 60 deg frame. This depends on items 6 and 8, because at EV -6 bright stars clip further and the cube's texels show.

## 4. A distant body blinks dark and changes colour and shape where the impostor hands over to geometry

- **Severity:** major (photo: major, artifact: major)
- **Screenshots:** `out/screens/sky/crescent-earth-approach-sheet.png`, `out/screens/sky/crescent-earth-lunar-distance.png`
- **Checked:** on the sheet the first four tiles are bright blue-white round dots, the fifth is a two-pixel grey speck, and the sixth and seventh are dim grey crescents. My re-measured per-tile sums show the same dip (the fifth tile is roughly a tenth of the fourth) and the same colour flip. `crescent-earth-lunar-distance.png` shows the dim grey crescent alone.
- **Defect:** Approaching a crescent Earth (phase 125 deg), the body changes brightness, colour and shape in jumps, although the sheet equalises the exposure for distance and every tile should be equally bright. Display-linear luma integrated over the body, per tile: 7.1, 7.5, 8.6, 7.9 for the impostor tiles (0.5-4.2 px), then 0.88 at 6.6 px (9 times darker), 4.4 at 13 px, 5.5 at 21 px. At the same step the colour flips from blue-white (peak 228, 235, 243; R:G:B 0.80 : 1 : 1.35) to neutral grey (peak 106, 104, 101; 1.05 : 1 : 0.95) and the shape from a full round blob to a thin crescent. Spec log of this run: "TAA keeps 99 % at 4.2 px, 34 % at 6.6 px, 76 % at 13.2 px, 89 % at 20.7 px". The same mechanism reaches the Moon in the main mission: 0.52 deg is 3.5-5.6 px at 720 internal lines for 60-85 deg fields and up to about 9 px at 1080 lines, so the Moon is a round blob with no phase shape, and zooming the cockpit view (25-85 deg, about 15 px at 25 deg) carries it through the hand-over. The Earth does the same in the map view. How dark the dip gets depends on the phase; thin crescents lose most.
- **Real reference:** In spacecraft imagery a shrinking Earth or Moon keeps its phase shape down to two or three pixels: a crescent 4-7 px across is already an elongated, off-centre sliver, not a round dot. Its total brightness and colour change smoothly (Earth stays blue-white, the Moon grey). Nothing fades out at an intermediate distance and comes back.
- **Likely cause:** (a) `src/render/sky/photometry.ts` `impostorWeight()`: the fade is driven by the diameter (`IMPOSTOR_FULL_PX` 3, `IMPOSTOR_NONE_PX` 6), so a crescent whose lit width is 1.4 px is handed to geometry that the core's temporal anti-aliasing resolve cannot hold (`src/render/core/post/postProcess.ts`). `docs/requests/sky-lighting-1.md` is "Status: declined" and its resolution advises handing over on lit width; the hand-off (lines 23, 229, 320, 346; sections 6.1 and 9) still calls the request open. (b) `src/render/sky/feature.ts` `prepareImpostors()`: flux is a Lambert sphere times `geometricAlbedo` (`lambertSphereIlluminance`) and colour is `meanColor` over its luma, which match neither the rendered body's phase curve nor its colour (here the lab Earth without atmosphere); the hand-off puts the Lambert Moon at several times the real one at quadrature. (c) `impostorSigmaPx()` makes the blob a round Gaussian at every phase. (d) `diameterPx` uses `frame.renderSize`, the internal resolution, so dynamic resolution moves a body in and out of the impostor band.
- **Fix direction:** Follow the core-support resolution: hand over on lit width, D / 2 x (1 + cos(phase)), not on diameter. The critics suggest full weight until the lit width passes 2-3 px with a fade over the next 2-3 px; note that the log shows only 76 % kept at a lit width of 2.8 px and 89 % at 4.4 px, so set the thresholds from the measurement (a fixed 12-16 px diameter is not enough for thin phases). Make the impostor an analytic far level of detail instead of a blob: a lit sphere ray-cast inside the impostor quad (terminator from the body's sun direction, `albedoLowRes` or mean colour, coverage anti-aliased by the pixel footprint, convolved with the star profile of item 6), so it carries the phase shape and does not depend on the resolve. Take the phase curve and disc colour per body from data so flux and colour match the rendered body at the hand-over, and check that against the real terrain and atmosphere renderers. Compute the size in display pixels, or add hysteresis, so resolution scaling cannot switch the representation. Replace the logged kept fraction by an assertion on the tonemapped sheet: integrated flux within 15 % step to step and chromaticity within a small tolerance from 0.5 to 21 px. Correct the hand-off.

## 5. The ghosts are bright, flat pastel discs with a red outline

- **Severity:** major (photo: major, artifact: minor)
- **Screenshots:** `out/screens/sky/flare-off-axis-35deg-ev12.png`, `out/screens/sky/flare-sun-just-outside-frame-ev12.png`, `out/screens/sky/flare-deep-space-ev12.png`, `out/screens/sky/sun-at-earth-limb-leo-flare.png`
- **Checked:** visible in all four. Re-measured ghost centres in the off-axis view: (174, 163, 178), (217, 209, 193), (223, 231, 234) and (85, 101, 86), the large one with a dark red-brown rim of about (70, 46, 0). Over the night-side Earth in the limb view: (161, 151, 136) and (163, 174, 178).
- **Defect:** The four ghosts are perfect circles (ellipses off axis) exactly on one line, each flat-filled in a different pastel colour, each with the same crisp concentric orange-red outline (rim within about 2 px) and an inner flat disc plus a lighter annulus, like targets or stickers. They are bright, not faint: 173-234/255 at EV 12 (the third is nearly white). With the sun just outside the frame, three of them hang at full strength in an otherwise black frame. It is the look of a 1990s lens-flare plug-in.
- **Real reference:** Real ghosts are images of the iris: polygons with the blade count (heptagons for this 7-blade lens, aligned with the rays) or cut to a cat's eye off axis, with soft defocused edges and uneven interiors (caustic arcs, brighter cusps). They differ widely in size and brightness, usually a dozen or more of which most are barely visible, mostly large and very dim, tinted by the coatings at low saturation (green, magenta, amber). Colour fringing is lateral, red on one side and blue on the other along the line through the image centre, not a ring all round. At an exposure for sunlit subjects they sit several stops under mid-grey and vanish over a lit Earth; many NASA frames with the sun in them show none.
- **Likely cause:** `src/render/sky/flare.ts` `GHOSTS` (4 entries): energies 6e-5 to 1.2e-4 of the sun's illuminance into radii of 0.016-0.08 rad. Example: 130,000 lx x 6e-5 / (pi x 0.016^2) is about 9,700 cd/m^2, twice mid-grey at EV 14.4, where a coated two-reflection ghost is of order 2.5e-5 over 0.05 rad or more, about 400 cd/m^2. `ghostProfile()` and the ghost branch of `FLARE_WGSL`: `disc = saturate((1 - r (1 +- shift)) / soft)` scales each channel about the ghost's own centre, which draws the concentric red outline; `disc * (0.55 + 0.45 * smoothstep(0.6, 1, r))` makes the annulus; the profile is circular only.
- **Fix direction:** Give the ghosts the aperture's shape: a 7-sided mask turned with the spike set (from the same blade data), slightly curved edges, convolved with the solar disc so the edge is at least a solar radius soft, clipped by a second circle off axis. Make the chroma a per-channel shift of the ghost centre along the axis-to-sun line instead of radial scaling. Fill unevenly (a gradient along that line, a faint edge caustic). Cut the energies about 5-10 times and enlarge all but one, so no ghost exceeds roughly a quarter of mid-grey at the sunlit exposure and the total stays at or under the present 0.036 %. Prefer more, mostly sub-threshold ghosts with a spread of radii (some far larger than the frame at very low luminance) to four equal-looking ones. Keep `flareRadiance` and the specs in step.

## 6. Every star is the same hard little dot, so constellations do not read

- **Severity:** major (photo: major, artifact: minor)
- **Screenshots:** `out/screens/sky/orion-closeup-ev-5.png`, `out/screens/sky/crux-centaurus-ev-5.png`, `out/screens/sky/starfield-deep-space-ev-5.png`, `out/screens/sky/orientation-sirius.png`, `out/screens/sky/orientation-alnitak.png`, `out/screens/sky/orientation-acrux.png`, `out/screens/sky/leo-night-dark-adapted.png`
- **Checked:** visible in all seven. I could find Orion in the close-up only from the critics' coordinates, and in the orientation views the named star is a dot barely larger than its neighbours. Re-measured: Betelgeuse and Rigel cover 23 and 24 px at 128/255 or more.
- **Defect:** The star field is uniform "salt". In the Orion close-up Betelgeuse (V 0.5) and Rigel (V 0.1) are blobs about 5.5 px across, the belt stars about 4.5 px, ordinary V 4-6 stars 1-4 px. Betelgeuse is a white core with a one-pixel orange rim. Orion cannot be picked out without working out where it should be (belt at the frame centre, Betelgeuse at 490,107, Rigel at 793,574); alpha and beta Centauri and the Southern Cross do not stand out in the Crux view either. Bright stars get no glare at all while the sun, through the same lens, gets a 200 px halo and 14 spikes: the lens model is inconsistent between features.
- **Real reference:** In a long exposure, and to the eye, a star's image grows steadily with its brightness because lens and eye point-spread functions have wide wings. First-magnitude stars are saturated discs several times wider than faint stars, with a soft glow that keeps the star's colour, so constellations are obvious at once. Betelgeuse is orange across its whole image, Rigel blue-white.
- **Likely cause:** `src/render/sky/photometry.ts` and `STARS_WGSL` in `src/render/sky/shaders.ts`: one Gaussian of sigma 0.7 px (`STAR_PSF_SIGMA_PX`) in a fixed 6 x 6 px quad (`STAR_QUAD_SIGMAS` 4.3), capped by `capAdditive(500)`. A pure Gaussian has no wings, so the visible radius grows only as sqrt(ln(flux)): six magnitudes change the clipped radius by about 1.5 px, and a clipped core is white whatever the catalogue colour. The lens's glare function (`haloProfile` in `flare.ts`) is applied to the sun only, and the core's bloom is too weak to stand in for wings.
- **Fix direction:** Use one point-spread function with wings for every point source: the Gaussian core plus a skirt, with the skirt's energy taken out of the core so the lattice sum still equals E / Omega_px. The critics offer two skirts: a Moffat-type profile (beta about 2-2.5, 10-20 % of the energy outside the core), or the flare's own normalised glare profile at its energy fraction (about 0.2 % of the star's flux), which keeps stars and sun on the same lens. Choose the one that meets the display check below. Size each star's quad from its pre-exposed flux (out to where the skirt drops under about 1/1024) so only the few dozen to few hundred bright stars at the current exposure get large quads; the skirt carries the catalogue colour. Use the same profile for impostors (item 4). This is photometry, not a brightness knob. Keep the photometry spec (integrated flux within 5 %) and add a display check: visible diameter grows monotonically with flux and is at least three times larger at V 0 than at V 5 at EV -5. Do this together with item 9, which changes the same sigma.

## 7. Faint diffuse light changes hue: the Milky Way goes rust-brown and the Large Magellanic Cloud purple

- **Severity:** minor (photo: minor; the artifact critic folded it into its major night-sky finding)
- **Screenshots:** `out/screens/sky/milky-way-band-ev-3.png` (against `out/screens/sky/starfield-deep-space-ev-5.png`), `out/screens/sky/leo-night-dark-adapted.png`
- **Checked:** the band at EV -3 is plainly dark red-brown. Re-measured brightest 21 px patch: (48, 29, 23), R/G 1.63 and R/B 2.07, against (113, 85, 74), R/G 1.33 and R/B 1.52, for the bulge at EV -5. At a gain of six the Large Magellanic Cloud in `leo-night-dark-adapted.png` is purple, (2.9, 1.8, 3.9).
- **Defect:** The Milky Way changes hue with exposure and turns rust-brown as it dims. At EV -3 the bright part of the bulge is (26, 15, 11), R/G 1.7 and R/B 2.4-3.0, and the band averages 1 : 0.54 : 0.37; at EV -5 the bulge is a warm grey (62, 51, 47), 1 : 0.81 : 0.73. At the floor exposure the Large Magellanic Cloud is purple.
- **Real reference:** A dim Milky Way is colourless silver-grey to the dark-adapted eye and neutral to warm white in an underexposed photograph. Faint diffuse light never looks saturated red or purple, and it does not go red as it gets fainter.
- **Likely cause:** The per-channel toe of the core's AgX tonemap (the weaker channels reach black first) and the look's extra saturation in `src/render/core/post/tonemap.ts`, acting on the warm colour of the source map. Not in the sky code.
- **Fix direction:** Same request to core-support as the toe in item 2: treat the toe on luminance, or desaturate towards neutral at very low levels, and remove the look's extra contrast and saturation below about 4 stops under mid-grey. Then re-shoot and require channel ratios at the floor exposure within 20 % of the EV -5 image. Lowering the dark floor (item 3) moves the band out of the toe and removes most of it.

## 8. Faint stars from the 1024 cube are rectangular smudges beside pin-sharp catalogue stars

- **Severity:** minor (photo: minor, artifact: minor)
- **Screenshots:** `out/screens/sky/orion-closeup-ev-5.png` (against `out/screens/sky/orion-closeup-ev-5-mw2048.png`), `out/screens/sky/milky-way-1024-vs-2048-orion-crop-3x.png`
- **Checked:** in the crop sheet the 1024 side shows soft, oblong blue and orange smudges where the 2048 side shows points; a 3 x crop of `orion-closeup-ev-5.png` shows the smudges between sharp catalogue stars. I also looked for a periodic lattice in the background spectrum and found none, as the artifact critic reports.
- **Defect:** With the shipped default (Milky Way cube uploaded from 1024 per face) the stars fainter than the catalogue are magnified 2.6 times in the 24 deg view (the cockpit zooms to 25 deg) and become axis-aligned, partly oblong smudges 3-8 px across: two different kinds of star in one picture. Column autocorrelation of the background at lags 2 / 3 is 0.57 / 0.27 with 1024 against 0.43 / 0.13 with 2048. The hand-off calls the cost "invisible at a 70 deg field", but that was measured at 720p: at the 1440p target and 60 deg a pixel is 0.042 deg against a 0.088 deg texel, the same 2.1 times magnification over the whole sky in normal play (3.1 times at 4K). It will show more once the dark exposure is lowered (item 3).
- **Real reference:** A real star field has one continuous population of points at every brightness, sharper and more numerous towards the faint end, over a smooth diffuse glow. No blocks.
- **Likely cause:** `src/render/sky/feature.ts` `loadSky()`: `limit = options.milkyWayFaceSize ?? MILKY_WAY_FACE_SIZE` (1024, 0.088 deg per texel) skips the finest baked mip to stay inside the 0.1 GB VRAM line, and `BACKGROUND_FS` samples it bilinearly. The catalogue stops at V 6.5 (8,920 stars), so V 6.5-10 stars live in the texture. `docs/requests/sky-lighting-2.md` is "declined, escalate: lead" and undecided; the hand-off still lists it as open.
- **Fix direction:** Three routes. (1) Move the stars down to about V 9-10 into the instanced catalogue (tens of thousands of instances; the background pass was measured at 0.013 ms of its 0.08 ms budget) and bake them out of the cube, so the cube carries only diffuse glow, which survives magnification; the photo critic prefers this, and it needs the sky bake. (2) Fit 2048 into the same memory: transcode the chain to BC6H where `texture-compression-bc` is available (8 bits per texel against 32 for rgb9e5, so the full 2048 chain is about 34 MB, the size of today's 1024 chain) with the 1024 rgb9e5 chain as fallback. (3) The lead raises the VRAM line and 2048 ships. At the least, use a bicubic or Gaussian magnification filter so texels are not rectangles.

## 9. Stars shimmer for a static camera in vacuum

- **Severity:** minor (artifact only)
- **Screenshots:** `out/screens/sky/leo-night-dark-adapted.png`
- **Checked:** not confirmable from a still. I opened the screenshot and it shows nothing about shimmer either way, so the item is kept on the critic's evidence, the log of `tests/browser/sky/stars.spec.ts` "static camera", which I did not re-run. I did confirm that the spec asserts the loose bounds (lines 413-415: mean under 3 %, worst under 15 %) and that `docs/requests/sky-lighting-1.md` is declined with the sigma advice quoted below.
- **Defect:** Log of this run: "resolved (TAA), 515 star pixels above 0.05: consecutive frames differ by 0.82 % on average, 7.5 % at most; same jitter phase 8 frames apart: 2.81 % at most". That is a 7.5 Hz ripple on the rim pixels of every star (the builder measured up to 11/255). The acceptance criterion (frame difference zero) is still not met, and the spec asserts the failing behaviour.
- **Real reference:** Outside an atmosphere stars are perfectly steady.
- **Likely cause:** A sigma 0.7 px Gaussian under the core's jittered 3 x 3 reconstruction (`src/render/core/post/postProcess.ts`).
