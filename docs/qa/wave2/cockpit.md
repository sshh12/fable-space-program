## 1. The whole cabin reads as an untextured clay render: nothing reflects, every part is one flat tone

- **Severity:** blocker (photo)
- **Screenshots:** `pad-noon-forward-window.png`, `night-displays-floods.png`, `hatch-mechanism.png`, `look-back-couches-hatch.png`, `reentry-plasma.png`; the integrated scene shows the same: `out/screens/integration/budget-leo-cockpit.png`.
- **Checked:** visible in all six. Ring frames, stringers, close-out panels, lockers, hatch door and console are each a single matte grey or beige with hard edges; no surface shows a window highlight or a gloss change. Slightly less absolute than written: the black window bezels carry a thin bright rim line (pad view), the duct at the top left of the pad view has a soft gradient and the lever tips in `closeup-switches.png` have a small highlight, so a specular lobe exists, but nothing shows an image of its surroundings.
- **Defect:** Parts declared metallic (aluminium ring frames and stringers, steel fasteners, handholds, the "chrome" switch levers) look like flat grey paint or go black. The baked detail layers, edge wear and grime are invisible beyond about 0.3 m; where wear shows it is a sprinkle of separate dots (item 21). No scratch, scuff, fingerprint, placard density, stowage or cable slack at seat distance. Local contrast is low everywhere.
- **Real reference:** Apollo CM, Orion and Crew Dragon cabin photographs: painted aluminium with visible sheen and soft window highlights that slide across it, brushed and anodised metal with streaked highlights, continuous bright wear lines on handled edges, scuffs around latches, dense placards, Velcro grids, stowage bags, cable bundles with slack. A metal frame next to a window shows the bright window and the dark console in it.
- **Likely cause:** `src/render/cockpit/shaders.ts` `shade()`: there is no reflection of the cabin for glossy and metallic surfaces. Line 833 `ambientSpec = amb.uniformE * (f0 * 0.5 + 0.02) * (1.0 - roughness) / PI` is a direction-less constant and `cabinLight()` adds one representative-point lobe per window or flood only. A metallic = 1 material therefore has no diffuse and a flat specular: uniform grey (aluminium 0.91) or black (steel levers). Half of the diffuse light is the direction-less integrating-sphere term `amb.uniformE * occ.ao * cavity` (line 834), so the detail normal maps (detailStrength 0.5-0.8 in `data/defs/visuals/cockpits/kestrel.json`) shade nothing. The detail set is 8 procedural 512^2 layers; none of the 50 USD texture budget was spent (the hand-off lists this under known weaknesses).
- **Fix direction:** 1) Give the cabin a specular environment: render the interior into a small cube map (128-256^2 from the cabin centre, re-rendered when sun, floods or attitude change, prefiltered mips, parallax-corrected to the cone) and use it for every interior material's specular and for the glass reflections (item 8). 2) Replace the constant `uniformE` with a directional ambient (SH, or a per-vertex bent normal with the window, patch and flood polygons) so normals and detail maps shade (item 6). 3) Replace the procedural detail layers with photographed or generated PBR sets for painted aluminium, anodise, beta cloth and Nomex at higher contrast, and add the missing clutter (stowage bags, cable slack, tape, placard density). Re-shoot every seat view and compare side by side with an Orion or Apollo cabin photograph.

## 2. The insulation blankets are bevelled boxes with a net drawn on them

- **Severity:** blocker (both lenses)
- **Screenshots:** `pad-noon-forward-window.png` (x 130-430, y 150-420; x 620-720, y 480-560), `leo-sun-patch-console.png` (x 430-560, y 100-250; x 150-450, y 380-560), `night-displays-floods.png` (x 0-470, y 200-640), `reentry-plasma.png` (whole wall), `closeup-switches.png` (x 0-530, y 90-340), `closeup-fdai.png` (left third), `perf-1440p-leo-seat.png`, `out/screens/integration/budget-leo-cockpit.png`.
- **Checked:** visible in every one. Each bay shows a lighter quadrilateral inside a darker margin, bounded by straight creases that cut across the quilt lines; the quilt is a diamond grid of soft dark bands with no relief. In the night view the lines bend across the bays.
- **Defect:** The blankets fill about half of every seat view and read as flat cardboard with a line pattern projected on it. (1) Flat-topped boxes with a hard bevel: the lighter rectangle looks like a semi-transparent sheet laid over the bay. (2) The seams are soft dark bands 1-2 cm wide with no stitch, thread or groove, so in the close-ups the wall looks out of focus. No cell bulges, nothing pinches at the seams, no wrinkle or sag, no sheen; at night the grid warps like a stretched projection.
- **Real reference:** Quilted Nomex and beta-cloth liners (Soyuz orbital module, Shuttle middeck, Apollo, Orion): every cell is a small pillow with its own highlight and shadow, the seam is a 1-3 mm stitched groove with thread rows and puckers radiating from it, the cloth wrinkles toward tie-downs and sags between them, the edge rolls under the frame and is laced or snapped down. Often the liner is smooth fabric with snaps, Velcro and labels.
- **Likely cause:** `src/render/cockpit/cabin/shell.ts` `blanket()` lines 437-453: one patch on a 4 cm grid with `edge(x) = min(1, d * 6) ** 0.5`, `d = min(x, 1 - x) * 2`: a plateau with a ramp only in the outer 8 % of the bay (about one mesh cell, C1 kink at the clamp), and `aoAt` ramps 0.72 to 1.0 over the same cell: a one-cell ramp against a flat plateau gives the crease. The quilting is not geometry: `shaders.ts` `materialDetail` case 1u (lines 686-707) uses `puff = smoothstep(0.0, 0.11 * fill, seam)`, `d.cavity *= mix(0.7, 1.0, puff)` and `d.grime = max(d.grime, (1.0 - puff) * 0.6)` (albedo x (1 - 0.45 x grime)), which paints a dark band about 15 mm wide along every seam; the slope term (0.22) is too weak to read under the direction-less cabin light and the stitch term is removed by `aaFade(140 / cell)` at seat distance.
- **Fix direction:** Model the relief. Tessellate blankets to about 1 cm at lod0 (135 k triangles of the budget are unused) and displace each diamond cell with a C1-continuous pillow profile (per-cell fill as now), pinched at seams and tufts, plus low-frequency sag and wrinkle noise; roll the rim smoothly over at least 3 vertex rows and take the rim darkening from the occlusion bake, not from a linear ramp. Make the seam a 1-3 mm groove with a sharp normal crease and a thread row that survives filtering at 0.5-1.5 m, cut the seam darkening to 10-15 % or less, and bake a wrinkle normal layer that radiates from the stitches (`tools/cockpit/bake-textures.ts`). Shade with the cloth BRDF and a weave normal so the cloth has sheen. Add lacing or snaps along the rim and vary bays (some smooth panels with snaps and Velcro) so the wall is not one pattern.

## 3. The sun patch on the console has torn, comb-shaped edges

- **Severity:** blocker (both lenses)
- **Screenshots:** `closeup-mfd.png` (x 1340-1500, y 150-420; x 0-170, y 760-860), `closeup-fdai.png` (x 920-1140, y 770-890), `closeup-switches.png` (x 1020-1180, y 0-110), `leo-sun-patch-console.png` (x 1195-1290, y 360-470; x 810-850, y 560-600), `perf-1440p-leo-seat.png` (same patch, stale).
- **Checked:** visible in all five, at the seat's normal field of view as well as in the close-ups. The edge cast by the window aperture is clean (the straight diagonal across MFD 1 from about (380,330) to (800,120) in `closeup-mfd.png`); every edge that is the shadow of an interior part is a comb.
- **Defect:** The brief's "hard sun patch on the console" is the brightest, highest-contrast feature of the hero seat view, and wherever its edge is the shadow of an interior part (raised centre section, MFD bezel, FDAI housing) the boundary breaks into fingers 2-4 cm long with fine parallel streaks inside them, all pointing along the sun's direction on the panel, like torn paper or stretched shadow-map texels.
- **Real reference:** Sunlight through a spacecraft window (Apollo CM, ISS cupola, Crew Dragon): the patch is the window's outline with a clean edge, and the shadow of a part 5-20 cm from the receiver is a straight, sharp line with a penumbra of 0.5-2 mm (distance x 0.0093). No teeth, no streaks, identical from frame to frame.
- **Likely cause:** The cabin's own sun map at grazing incidence; the two critics give two mechanisms that the stills do not separate, and both can act together. (a) Resolution (artifact): `src/render/cockpit/sunmap.ts` `sunMapTransform` (lines 31-38) and `feature.ts` lines 469 and 884 fit one 2048^2 orthographic map to the bounding sphere of the whole interior (2 mm texels) although the sun exists only inside the window beams; the sun arrives through the side-right window at 10-15 degrees to the console face, so one texel covers 7 mm to several cm on the receiver along the light and straight occluder edges rasterise as texel rows. (b) Self-occlusion (photo): the caster pipeline (`sunmap.ts` lines 88-89) uses `cullMode: 'none'` and no `depthBias` or `depthBiasSlopeScale`, and the console face that receives the patch is itself a caster (`feature.ts` line 567); in `shaders.ts` `sunMapVisibility()` (lines 503-546) the bias is at most 3.5 texels of depth plus a 3.5 texel normal offset (lines 516-517) while a texel spans 8-12 mm of depth at this angle. Away from any occluder the blocker search finds nothing and returns 1 (line 531), so the patch interior is clean; near a real shadow edge the 8 x 8 PCF (which reaches only 4 texels) compares neighbouring texels of the receiver's own plane against one reference depth.
- **Fix direction:** Tell the two apart first: draw the map with front faces culled (if the teeth go, it is (b)); halve the fitted radius (if the teeth halve, it is (a)). Then fix structurally, not by tuning constants. Spend the resolution where the sun is: fit the map (or one tile per sun-facing window) to the cross-section of the window-aperture prism in sun space (about 0.35 x 0.6 m, 0.2-0.3 mm texels at the same 2048^2), cull front faces or add `depthBiasSlopeScale`, make the shader bias proportional to the tangent of the incidence (clamped) and compute the receiver-plane slope `dzduv` analytically from the geometric normal and the map axes, not from screen derivatives. Or drop the map for the sun term and trace one shadow ray per lit pixel against the lod0 BVH that already exists on the GPU for the occlusion bake (only pixels with `sunThroughWindows > 0` pay). Keep the analytic aperture. Add a browser test with the sun at 8, 15 and 30 degrees to the console face that asserts the lit fraction is monotonic across the patch edge along three scan lines. Acceptance: in `closeup-mfd.png` every patch edge is straight within 1 px.

## 4. What is seen through the windows is placeholder: no reviewed evidence that any window view works over the real world

- **Severity:** blocker (photo; artifact rates it major and "not a player-facing defect of the interior, but the evidence is missing")
- **Screenshots:** `moon-horizon.png`, `leo-earth-side-window.png`, `chase-window-sun.png`, `pad-noon-forward-window.png`.
- **Checked:** visible in all four. Moon: a flat grey area (181,184,184) cut from black by one perfectly straight line. Earth: a pale blue-white field (about 196,212,227) with a ruler-straight white limb band; "no cloud" is slightly too strong, there are a few faint blurred cloud shapes, but no coast, ocean or structure, and the cabin around it is black. Chase: a flat grey cone against a flat blue field with a black hole for the window. Pad: white-blue blank outside, no sky, tower or cloud.
- **Defect:** Four of the brief's reviewed acceptance views show exactly what PROMPT.txt bans (blue gradients, smooth spheres, placeholder art). As screenshots they read as two-tone graphics, and they cannot show whether the glass, the dust and the reflections sit correctly over a real, detailed view. The exposure compensation chosen for the stand-ins (`ec=-3`, `ec=-2` in `tests/browser/cockpit/views.spec.ts` lines 31 and 37) leaves the cabin black and the subject pale.
- **Real reference:** Apollo LM window photographs (cratered, sharply shadowed regolith to an uneven horizon); ISS and Apollo photographs of Earth through a window (cloud systems, ocean glint, coastlines, a thin layered blue limb, the window frame as a dark silhouette with rim light); exterior photographs of Dragon and Orion windows (Earth and sky mirrored in the glass, sun glints, lit bezel and shade inside).
- **Likely cause:** Lab stand-ins, not the cockpit's shaders: `labs/cockpit/main.ts` line 235 builds the scene from `createLabPlanetFeature()` (low-resolution albedo sphere), `createLabSkyFeature()` (`labs/cockpit/sky.ts`) and `createLabHullFeature()` (`labs/cockpit/hull.ts`, one material 'backshell' [0.72, 0.73, 0.72]); hand-off deviation 7 says so.
- **Fix direction:** Shoot the window views where the real world exists: `labs/integration` draws the cockpit since commit 346aa55. Add the pad, LEO Earth, Moon horizon, reentry and both chase views there (a spec under `tests/browser/cockpit` that opens the integration lab with the seat camera, or a request to the integration owner), look at them, and fix what the real scene shows. Expose as a photographer would at the exposure the game uses: for Earth or the Moon expose for the subject and let the window frame go to a dark silhouette with rim light; for the cabin let the window clip. Keep the stand-in lab for functional tests only and stop presenting its frames as the acceptance screenshots.

## 5. The night chase view is 98 % black and its screenshot spec fails

- **Severity:** blocker (photo; artifact rates it major)
- **Screenshots:** `chase-window-night.png`.
- **Checked:** visible. 86 % of the pixels are exactly 0,0,0 and only 2.2 % are above 8/255, which agrees with the reported failure `chase-window-night: mostly black (0.019)` against `minNonBlack: 0.05` (`tests/browser/cockpit/views.spec.ts` line 57). With a 12x gain the cabin behind the glass appears, the hull stays at 0 and one flood lens is the only bright thing in frame.
- **Defect:** Two dim window shapes float in a void: the hull has no silhouette, there are no stars and no Earth night side, no light spills from the lit cabin onto the window wells, the bezels or the nearby hull, the glass carries no reflection, and the lit cabin is barely above black. Both critics report 18 functional tests passing and this one failing on the current tree.
- **Real reference:** A lit crewed capsule on the night side (Crew Dragon and Soyuz filmed from the ISS): the windows glow, warm or cool by lamp type, and are the brightest thing in frame; their light spills onto the well, the bezel and the nearby hull; the hull is faintly readable in earthshine or moonlight against stars and city lights; the glass carries reflections.
- **Likely cause:** A regression from outside the package after the shot was last tuned: render-core commit aa8fd79 ("a lit area brighter than the prior sets the exposure from 2 % of the frame", `src/render/core/post/exposure.ts`, 2026-11-10 21:46, after the last cockpit commit). The directly visible flood lens now sets the exposure and the shot's `ec=-2` (`views.spec.ts` line 46: scene=night, distance 1.6 m, fov 45, windows about 2 % of the frame) pushes the cabin under black. Independently, the cabin's light is written as emission on interior pixels only (`shaders.ts` `shade()`, hand-off deviation 2), so nothing outside the pressure wall receives it; the well walls belong to the exterior; the lab hull has no night ambient.
- **Fix direction:** Re-tune the shot for the new metering (`ec` 0, no bare lamp in view, distance about 0.7 m so the window fills a quarter of the frame). Light the window wells and the outer bezel from the cabin (treat each pane as an emitter toward the outside with the cabin's mean radiance times the glass transmittance). Then check the same view in the integration lab with the real exterior, stars and Earth night side. If the core's highlight rule under-exposes interiors seen through glass, file a request against the exposure governor instead of compensating per shot. The spec must pass without lowering its threshold.

## 6. Cabin light has no direction, falloff or cast shadow, and night is displayed brighter than noon

- **Severity:** major (both lenses)
- **Screenshots:** `night-displays-floods.png`, `pad-noon-forward-window.png`, `reentry-plasma.png`, `hatch-mechanism.png` (linkage rods and handle over the door and the window).
- **Checked:** visible. The night view has no pool of light under a flood, no dark corner, no shadow from the console, ring frames or handholds, no display glow on the panel. The mean of the night frame is 118/255 against 79/255 for the pad at noon: the "lit only by displays and floodlights" scene is the brighter picture. In the hatch view the rods and the handle cast nothing on the door.
- **Defect:** The floods and windows light the cabin like an ambient term. Nothing thinner than the bake spacing casts a shadow (hatch rods, handle, hand controllers, switch levers, handholds), the 4.5 cm deep ring frames cast nothing onto the blankets, the far wall is nearly as bright as the wall beside a window, and every scene is exposed to about the same brightness, so night looks like an evenly lit office. With the flat materials of item 1 this is what makes the pad, night and reentry views read as a clay render.
- **Real reference:** Apollo, Shuttle and Orion night-pass photographs: a few lamps make bright pools with fast falloff, the rest of the cabin sinks into shadow, displays tint nearby surfaces, the picture is low-key. Flood tubes are 20 cm strip sources 0.5-1 m away: soft but distinct shadows (a few cm of penumbra) under the console overhang, behind the controllers and under every rod. By day the surfaces next to a window are 5-10 times brighter than the opposite wall, with soft shadows of frames and couches.
- **Likely cause:** `src/render/cockpit/shaders.ts` `cabinLight` (lines 352-413): every source is an unoccluded Lambert polygon times one baked scalar per vertex (3.5 cm spacing, rest pose); floods share one lumen-weighted scalar for all six lamps (`occ.flood`, `occlusion.ts`), so no lamp casts a shadow of its own; windows use one radiance and per-vertex visibility. Line 834: the inter-reflection term `amb.uniformE * occ.ao * cavity` (`feature.ts` lines 963-964 with `lighting.cabinAlbedo` 0.5, so rho / (1 - rho) = 1) adds as much perfectly uniform light as the direct light delivers. Exposure: the core's interior metering adapts fully to the cabin mean, so a 5 cd/m^2 night cabin is displayed like a 500 cd/m^2 one.
- **Fix direction:** Give the fixed sources real visibility: the six floods never move relative to the cabin, so render small shadow maps (or one cube per pair, or one baked channel per lamp) at init and whenever a control moves, and shade them as the directional sources they are; give each bright window a shadow map along its axis, or trace BVH rays for the two or three dominant sources in a half-resolution pass. Replace the uniform inter-reflection irradiance with a baked first-bounce irradiance per source (the same lightmap as item 10), so the bounce has a gradient too. Ask the core for a luminance-dependent key for interior cameras (about -1.5 EV when the adapted mean is under 10 cd/m^2), or publish an exposure hint from the cockpit, so night looks like night and display glow shows.

## 7. Daylight leaks through the seam between the hatch door and its frame

- **Severity:** major (both lenses)
- **Screenshots:** `hatch-mechanism.png` (x 990-1310, y 320-520; x 1290-1390, y 500-580; arcs on the window ring at x 760-800, y 470-620); from the seat the door is at the centre of `look-back-couches-hatch.png`.
- **Checked:** visible. A broken blue-white line with bloom runs along the upper-right door edge, with pixels at 255,255,255. The thin bright arcs on the rim of the hatch-window ring are also there; from the still they could be the same leak or a rim highlight, so check them once the seam is closed.
- **Defect:** The outside is visible through the pressure hatch's perimeter: the seam glows, in segments, and blooms at the cabin's exposure.
- **Real reference:** A plug-type pressure hatch (Apollo unified hatch, Orion side hatch) seats on a continuous seal: the seam is a dark line (seal bead and contact shadow), never a light source.
- **Likely cause:** `src/render/cockpit/cabin/apertures.ts` `buildHatch` lines 212-227: the frame's seal ring starts at the opening outline and goes outward (`ring0 = loop3(g, base, 0)` to `ringSeal` at +14 mm), the door's edge wall starts at the outline inset by 4 mm (`doorLoop = offsetLoop(base, -0.004)`, `edge0` at depth 0.002). Nothing closes the 4 mm annulus between them, so the camera looks through the wall opening at whatever is behind; in the lab that is the back-face-culled stand-in hull, i.e. the sunlit sky.
- **Fix direction:** Close the annulus with geometry: a rubber seal bulb from the door edge to the seal land, or make the door a true plug whose rim overlaps the land on the cabin side, plus an outer door skin through the wall thickness so no exterior is ever visible around the door. Add a watertightness unit test: rays from several interior points toward a sphere of directions must all hit interior geometry or glass, and include the door in the closed shell the core cascades draw.

## 8. At night the window panes are flat mid-grey rectangles

- **Severity:** major (both lenses)
- **Screenshots:** `night-displays-floods.png` (panes at x 840-960, y 0-100 and x 1190-1330, y 30-120).
- **Checked:** visible. The panes measure a flat 112,112,111 and 106,107,106 (medians; 95th percentile within 4 levels).
- **Defect:** Both forward windows read as opaque grey plastic sheets, drawn shades or missing textures, not as glass in front of black space.
- **Real reference:** A cabin window at night is a dark mirror: black outside with stars or city lights, and over it a dim, sharp, slightly doubled reflection of the lit cabin (about 8 % per pane): the bright blankets, the flood tubes as lines, the displays as coloured rectangles.
- **Likely cause:** `src/render/cockpit/shaders.ts` `cabinRadianceAlong()` line 1092: `var L = amb.uniformE * cabin.bounce.w / PI * 1.6` is returned for every reflected ray that does not hit a display, flood or sun-patch polygon, and `glass()` (line 1211) adds it at `2.0 * F` for the two panes: about 13 % of the cabin's mean luminance as a flat value, which is 40 % display grey once the exposure has adapted to the cabin.
- **Fix direction:** Reflect an image, not a constant: sample the cabin reflection cube of item 1 (128-256^2, lod1, parallax-corrected against the cabin's cone) along the reflected ray, or a planar reflection for the pane in view; keep the exact display and flood polygon hits on top; add the second pane's offset ghost; remove the 1.6 factor. Verify against black space that the window shows stars through a faint cabin reflection.

## 9. The reentry view is the pad view with a sepia filter: clipped white windows, one uniform salmon tint

- **Severity:** major (both lenses)
- **Screenshots:** `reentry-plasma.png` (windows x 880-970, y 220-600 and x 1200-1380, y 330-620).
- **Checked:** visible. Both windows are featureless pure white with a faint salmon fringe; every surface has the same salmon tone (frame median 187,140,105) with no gradient away from the windows.
- **Defect:** A white source that lights orange. Nothing in the window says plasma: no colour, no streaming structure, no flicker cue. The glow does not fall off from the windows and there is no white cabin lighting to contrast with it.
- **Real reference:** Orion Artemis I, Soyuz and Crew Dragon entry footage from inside: the windows show streaming orange to pink-magenta glow with bright streaks and sparks, still coloured, not clipped; the glow pulses and washes over the window frames and the nearest surfaces only, while the cabin stays lit by its own white lamps and displays.
- **Likely cause:** `kestrel.json` `lighting.plasmaLuminance` 25000 with one colour [1.0, 0.42, 0.24] is added uniformly to every window's radiance (`shaders.ts` `windowRadiance` line 303, `cabin.plasma.rgb`) and reaches the cabin mostly through the uniform inter-reflection term, hence the even tint. The lab scene has the floods off (`labs/cockpit/main.ts` line 65, `reentry` `lights: false`), so it is the only light. The interior metering exposes for the cabin, so the windows are 6 or more stops over and every channel hits the tone curve's ceiling (hue lost). The plasma seen through the glass is the lab's stand-in.
- **Fix direction:** Meter interior views with a highlight term so window highlights keep colour during entry (the cabin goes darker, as in the footage), or request a hue-preserving highlight roll-off from the core. Drive each window's plasma radiance from the real plasma field with spatial and temporal variation (falloff from the windows, 5-15 Hz flicker, white-orange to pink) and let the direct window term dominate the tint (item 6). Turn the floods on in the entry scene so white and orange light mix. Re-shoot in the integration lab with the vfx plasma behind the glass.

## 10. Baked occlusion shows as stains that follow the mesh

- **Severity:** major (both lenses)
- **Screenshots:** `night-displays-floods.png` (around the FDAI x 500-520, y 330-530; left of MFD 1 x 700-760, y 280-300; whiskers left of each seven-segment readout at x 1105-1135, y 690-710, x 1225-1250, y 665-690, x 1170-1195, y 595-615; streaks right of the switch guards x 880-940, y 620-660); `closeup-switches.png` (blot around the FINE switch x 1000-1160, y 380-520); `closeup-fdai.png` (x 1150-1290, y 80-640); `hatch-mechanism.png` (radial spokes on the door around the window x 640-1000, y 400-860); `reentry-plasma.png` (console face); `chase-window-night.png` (starburst on the surface at the top of the right window).
- **Checked:** visible in all. The FINE switch sits in a soft black blot while its four identical neighbours have none; dark "speed lines" trail from each readout window and switch guard; cloudy smears surround the FDAI; the hatch door has light and dark spokes around its window. - **Screenshots:** `pad-noon-forward-window.png` (x 300-470, y 390-430), `reentry-plasma.png` (x 300-470, y 340-400).
- **Checked:** visible in both: the three-tube bundle ends with a flat cut face just past its clamp.
- **Defect:** The harness has no termination: no connector, grommet or slack.
- **Real reference:** A harness ends in a connector backshell or passes through a grommet, and sags slightly between clamps.
- **Likely cause:** `src/render/cockpit/cabin/fixtures.ts` lines 155-180: cable runs are defined by azimuth ranges with no termination.
- **Fix direction:** Terminate each cable run in the console body or a wall grommet with a backshell, and give the runs a little sag between clamps (this also gives the console of item 11 its cable feed).
