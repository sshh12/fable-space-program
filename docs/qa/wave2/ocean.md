## 1. Every whitecap near the camera is a soft white smudge

- **Severity:** blocker (photo: blocker, artifact: blocker). Merged from photo 1 and artifact 1.
- **Screenshots:** `perf-splash-1440p.png` (patch at native 640-940, 960-1150; streak at 2430, 915), `overcast.png` (410, 652) and (487, 670), `night.png` (100-125, 552), (110, 655), (1010-1030, 497), (1140, 470), `stability-animated.png` (20, 707), (160, 503), (365, 640), `splash.png` (1117, 457), (430, 610), `handover-refraction.png` (82, 427), `shoreAlong.png` (890, 563)
- **Checked:** visible in all seven. Zoomed: the `overcast.png` patch is a 30 x 18 px ellipse with a smooth fall-off and nothing inside. The `perf-splash-1440p.png` patch is about 300 px of airbrushed wisps with the wave shading showing through them. The `night.png` patches are smeared grey streaks. The photo critic lists the spot at (80, 427) under `refraction.png`; it is in `handover-refraction.png`.
- **Defect:** Every whitecap and foam patch within about 30 m of a 2-3 m camera is a soft-edged, partly transparent white smear with no internal structure: no bubbles, no lace, no holes, no streaking, no change of thickness. It reads as fog, or an out-of-focus sprite painted on the water, in the foreground of the splashdown close-up where the mission ends. With temporal anti-aliasing running (`stability-animated.png`) the patches smear further. The first review's "plastic foam" finding is still fully visible after its fix.
- **Real reference:** Recovery-ship and swimmer footage of Apollo, Orion and Dragon splashdowns, and any deck-level photograph. Fresh foam is opaque white with crisp ragged edges on the breaking crest. Within a second or two it opens into a sharp cellular net of bubble rafts with dark water showing through, stretched along the wave's travel and pulled into streaks downwind. Aged foam is a thin grey-green lace. Edges are crisp at pixel scale from 3 m, and foam hides the specular highlights beneath it. Under moonlight foam is a faint grey, far below the glitter. It is never a Gaussian blob.
- **Likely cause:** (a) Coverage exists only at the FFT cascades' texel size: 4.6 m (cascade 0), 1.1 m (cascade 1), 0.28 m (cascade 2), written in `src/render/ocean/shaders/fftWgsl.ts` lines 184-189 and sampled bilinearly in `src/render/ocean/shaders/surfaceWgsl.ts` lines 302-308, so near the camera the mask is a magnified blur. (b) The lace does not cut it (`surfaceWgsl.ts` lines 315-319): `laceN = saturate(0.5 + 0.3 * lace)` is itself a smooth band-limited field; the gate is `smoothstep(th - hw, th + hw, laceN)` with `hw = 0.08 + 0.6 * sqrt(laceLost)`, which widens wherever the lace is filtered, and `th` is clamped at `OCEAN_FOAM_CORE` 0.22 once `foamJ` exceeds 0.68, which `laceN` almost always clears, so the core is uniformly white (`bubbles` only varies 0.8-1.0). `min(foamJ * 3.0, 1.0)` turns the rim into a linear ramp of the blurred texture: the translucent halo through which the waves show. (c) Foam is shaded flat: `brdfWaterDirect` (`src/render/ocean/shaders/waterBrdf.ts` lines 329-332) adds foam albedo / PI x `max(dot(s.up, l), 0)`, using the planet's up vector, with no normal of its own and no thickness. (d) Temporal anti-aliasing smears what is left (`taaReactive` 0.45).
- **Fix direction:** Use the low-resolution foam field as a density, not as the picture. In the forward pass add a foam detail layer: two or three octaves of tiling cellular bubble lace (procedural Worley or a baked tile, periods about 2 m, 0.5 m and 0.12 m), anchored to the sea (advected in Lagrangian plane coordinates) and stretched along the local wave direction. Use the density as the threshold of that detail, `foam = smoothstep(1 - c, 1 - c + one pixel footprint, detail)`, so coverage is binary at pixel scale, thick foam is solid, thin foam is hard-edged lace, and the rim is a threshold instead of a ramp. Keep two states: the active breaker (thick, bright, along the crest) and residual foam that decays over 5-10 s through lace to nothing while it drifts downwind. Shade foam as a diffuse layer with its own bumpy normal and a thickness-dependent albedo (about 0.9 fresh, 0.3 lace), with no water specular through it, and give foam pixels the full reactive value. If the cascades' density is still too coarse near the camera, add a camera- or vessel-centred foam clipmap at 5 cm texels or finer. Keep the statistical term only where a pixel cannot resolve 0.5 m. Judge at 1440p from 3 m beside the capsule.

## 2. From 100 m to 3 km the sun path is a matt grey plate with dark worm lines and no sparkle

- **Severity:** blocker (photo: blocker, artifact: major). Merged from photo 2 and artifact 4.
- **Screenshots:** `alt500.png` (470-830, 430-670), `alt3k.png`, `foam-coverage-150m.png`, `climb-273m.png`, `handover-stray-water.png`, `night.png` (moon path, 450-850, 330-650), `markers-with-chop.png` (lower half), `midday.png` (380-900, 540-720), `out/screens/integration/budget-splashdown.png`
- **Checked:** visible in all of them; the zoomed `alt500.png` and `night.png` crops look like cracked plaster. Re-measured 99.5th percentile of luma: 138 in `alt500.png` (maximum 181), 132 in `alt3k.png` (maximum 143), 163 in `climb-273m.png`; `midday.png` at 2 m has 99.9th percentile 198 and maximum 241. Pixels under 45 % of their 5x5 median luma inside the glint: 215 in the `alt500.png` region (193 of them isolated, the darkest at 5 % of the median), 52 in `alt3k.png`, 55 in `markers-with-chop.png`, 25 in `midday.png`, 1053 in the moon path of `night.png` (the darkest at 2 %). My counts are higher than the artifact critic's (43, 23, 28, 14, 310), so treat those as a lower bound. In `budget-splashdown.png` 61 pixels in clusters of 1-4 are 40 or more levels bluer than their grey neighbours, for example (96, 109, 134) inside (130, 128, 127) at native (1021, 747). `markers-swell-only.png` shows the smooth lobe by itself: on a mirror-flat sea the sun's reflection is a broad grey fan.
- **Defect:** The sun path is a continuous matt grey sheet crossed by dark squiggly vein lines 1-2 px wide, with isolated single dark pixels inside it: embossed stucco straight down from 150 m, cracked plaster or crumpled foil at 500 m, a bump-mapped plastic sheet from 3 km. Near the camera the highlights are soft pale blobs. Nothing reaches white and no pixel clips, so there is no glitter at all, and the contrast is inverted against reality: a bright continuous ground with dark lines instead of dark water carrying brilliant points. The single dark pixels will flicker in motion.
- **Real reference:** Helicopter, aircraft and parachute-camera footage from 100 m to 3 km. Sun glitter is thousands of discrete images of the sun: pixel-sharp, saturated (2 % of 1.6e9 cd/m2 clips at any exposure), densest at the mirror point and thinning outward, twinkling from frame to frame, with dark blue water between them. From 500 m it is a fine sparkling grain with brighter and dimmer bands along the waves and no dark cracks; from 3 km a grainy, scintillating silver. It becomes a smooth sheen only from tens of kilometres. A moon path is made of short horizontal dashes.
- **Likely cause:** (a) Slope spikes: `surfaceWgsl.ts` line 371, `grad = ... e * (sx / max(jxx, 0.25) + rp.y) + f * (sy / max(jyy, 0.25) + rp.z)`. The per-axis division by the cascade Jacobian multiplies all summed slopes by up to 4 along pinched crests, including the capillary layers (lines 325-343) and the long-wave layer (lines 347-366), which that Jacobian never displaced. The facet leaves the lobe in thin lines and single texels: the veins and the dark pixels. (b) No sparkle statistics: `waterAlpha` (`waterBrdf.ts` lines 178-181) puts every slope the normal does not resolve into one smooth GGX lobe, so each pixel shows the ensemble mean. At 0.2-5 m footprints that is the capillary and short-gravity variance, about half of Cox-Munk, and with the data's `unresolvedSlopeVariance` 0.0025 the sun is smeared over several degrees. Physically a 1 m2 footprint holds a few hundred 5 cm facets and the chance that one mirrors the 0.53 deg sun is about 1e-4 at the peak: roughly 3 % of pixels should carry a sparkle about 30 times the mean at 500 m, about one per pixel at 3 km. (c) Temporal anti-aliasing averages what moves (`taaReactive` 0.45). (d) The lab presets lock EV 15 (`labs/ocean/main.ts` lines 45-50), which parks the mean glint at mid-grey (item 17).
- **Fix direction:** Apply the Jacobian only to the cascades' own slopes, with the full 2x2 inverse, and soft-limit the final slope (about 0.6, the Stokes limit) so no facet spikes. Add a stochastic glint term to `brdfWaterDirect` for the unresolved variance, in the manner of Chermain et al. or Zirr and Kaplanyan: per sea-anchored cell of about the footprint size, draw a Poisson count of facets that fall inside the sun's solid angle (mean = N_facets x D(h) x Omega_sun / 4, with N from the footprint area over a facet area of about (5 cm)^2), re-hashed about 15 times a second for twinkle; radiance = count x sun radiance x Fresnel x the facet's share. Keep the smooth lobe only as the expectation where the mean count is above about 5, so energy is unchanged; the same function still yields the broad orbital glint, where the mean count is huge. Lower the variance floor within about 20 m of the camera. Clamp through `writeHdr` and let bloom do the glare. Add an assertion that counts single-pixel outliers inside the glint. Re-shoot 150 m, 500 m and 3 km at the exposure the game's meter chooses.

## 3. From 5 to 30 km the sea is a gradient with a foggy smudge, and the wave texture stops along an arc

- **Severity:** blocker (photo: blocker, artifact: blocker). Merged from photo 3 and artifact 2.
- **Screenshots:** `alt10k.png`, `perf-10km-1440p.png`, `alt30k.png`, `climb-4796m.png`, `climb-16389m.png`, `handover-near-on.png`, `out/screens/integration/water-climb-all-14km.png`
- **Checked:** visible in all seven, weakest in `climb-4796m.png`, where the texture only thins out with range. Contrast-stretched copies of `alt10k.png` and `alt30k.png` make the arc and the blobs plain, and both show without stretching. Re-measured 99.5th percentile of luma: 115 in `alt10k.png`, 124 in `alt30k.png`, whose whole frame spans 58-128. `climb-16389m.png` is a gradient and a smudge and nothing else. The integration view at 14 km (taken before the fix round, under the real atmosphere) shows the same featureless grey glint column.
- **Defect:** The sea is a smooth blue-grey gradient with a blurry grey smudge where the glint should be; the glint peaks at mid-grey. At 10 km the lower 40 % of the frame carries a fine embossed wave texture with a compact bright spot at (560, 500), and above an arc at 10.5-15 km range it turns into smooth cloud-like blobs 40-80 px across: two different materials meeting along an arc, a disc of textured water on a blurred sheet. The screen centre of `alt10k.png` is 15.5 km away, exactly `fadeStart`. At 30 km one blob is a squarish noise cell with a hard outline at (685-690, 550-555). `PROMPT.txt` forbids blue gradients. The hand-off (section 11, "Detail fade") lists the texture change as a known weakness, but a player sees it in mid-screen during descent and ascent over water.
- **Real reference:** Airliner-window, high-altitude-balloon and booster-camera footage from 10-30 km. Saturated dark-blue water; a silver-white glint several times brighter than the sea around it (about 5-9 times at 7 m/s wind) that carries crisp fine structure everywhere: a fine-grained sparkle texture, parallel swell lines, wind rows and slick bands aligned with the wind, sharp-edged slicks and current fronts, ship wakes, cloud shadows. Outside the glint the sea shows swell texture and whitecap speckle. Haze is confined to the last degrees before the horizon. The character does not change with range, it only compresses; texture never stops at a fixed radius under the aircraft.
- **Likely cause:** (1) The far sheet is shaded without any wave structure. The contract-driven detail fade (`oceanDetail` in `src/render/ocean/shaders/waves.ts` line 216: 1 inside 0.7 x `fadeStart`, 0 at `fadeStart`; `nearFieldFadeStartM` 15000 and `detailFadeFraction` 0.7 in `earth.ocean.json`) removes every resolved normal (swell, long-wave layer, cascade 0) by distance, not by pixel footprint. Beyond it the deferred sheet has a flat normal and only the roughness field (`WATER_FIELD_WGSL`: `waterRoughnessFieldFixed`, `waterBrdf.ts` lines 84-117): isotropic gradient-noise octaves that change the lobe width by about +-35-40 % (`windVariability` 0.35, `gustVariability` 0.3, `slickStrength` 0.35), with a 1.4 : 1 downwind stretch that is invisible. Modulating the width of a broad lobe gives low-contrast blurry blobs; nothing tilts the mean normal, so there are no wave lines, although the 52-180 m swell and the long-wave slopes are resolvable at 10-40 m pixels. (2) The lab sky's in-scatter (`labs/ocean/labSky.ts`) and the fixed EV 15 flatten the contrast: glint to sea is 2.8 times in linear terms in `alt30k.png`. The integration view shows the lab sky is not the whole cause. (3) One wind speed (7 m/s) everywhere gives a broad Cox-Munk lobe whose peak is about mid-grey at EV 15. The photo critic notes that `out/screens/integration/budget-leo.png` and `budget-reentry-end.png` are acceptable once clouds and land carry the frame, so the fault is open water at 5-30 km.
- **Fix direction:** Keep wave-scale structure in the far sheet's shading. In `brdf_water`'s deferred path, evaluate the swell gradient analytically and sample a filtered slope-moment representation (mean slope plus variance, LEAN or LEADR style) of the long-wave layer in planet-fixed coordinates, both band-limited by the pixel footprint. Drive the forward detail fade by footprint as well, so near and far use the same normals at `fadeStart` and the 2 % hand-over criterion holds by construction. Make the roughness field strongly anisotropic and hard-edged: wind rows along the wind at 100-300 m spacing and slick bands at 5-10 : 1, slick boundaries from thresholded domain-warped noise instead of smoothstep blobs, and a real dynamic range (slope variance from about 0.008 in calm bands to 0.05; slick variance x 0.2-0.3). Replace or bound the lab sky's in-scatter, expose by meter in the lab, and re-judge 3, 10, 16 and 30 km in the integrated app with the real atmosphere and clouds.

## 5. The sea has no wind-sea waves: flat from 2 m, regular or directionless from the air

- **Severity:** major (photo: major, artifact: major). Merged from photo 11 and artifact 6.
- **Screenshots:** `midday.png`, `splash.png`, `stability-animated.png` (ruler-straight horizon), `foam-coverage-150m.png`, `alt500.png`, `alt3k.png`, `climb-273m.png` (parallel stripes at 600-950, 300-520)
- **Checked:** visible in all seven. From 2-3 m the horizon is a ruled line and no crest hides the base of the capsule or the water behind it (only the 1.1 m camera of `markers-with-chop.png` shows a slight bulge on the horizon). Straight down from 150 m the surface is isotropic blue sandpaper; at 3 km it is an orange-peel lattice of pillows; in `climb-273m.png` the long waves are evenly spaced diagonal stripes.
- **Defect:** From 2 m the sea is a nearly flat sheet with fine noise on top. From 150 m to 3 km the small scale is an isotropic bump field (lumps 10-20 m across, uniform from edge to edge) and the large scale is evenly spaced parallel stripes or a regular lattice. There are no crest lines of a wind sea and no direction a player could read the wind from. The hand-off lists the swell data as a known weakness (request `ocean-5`); it is still what a player sees.
- **Real reference:** A 7 m/s wind sea has a significant height of 1-1.6 m with short-crested, irregular waves in groups. From 2 m eye height nearby crests rise toward eye level, hide the troughs behind them and parts of a floating capsule, and make the horizon slightly ragged. Aerial photographs show roughly parallel, short-crested crest lines 30-80 m apart that break and merge, finer ripples lying across the wind, and from 3 km a clearly directional streaked texture.
- **Likely cause:** The drawn heights hold no wind sea. The spectrum is high-passed at 25 m (`highPassWavelengthM` in `earth.ocean.json`) while the JONSWAP peak for 7 m/s wind is near 70-76 m. That energy exists only as the four sinusoids of `data/defs/bodies/earth.json` `ocean.swell` (180, 95, 52, 28 m; amplitudes 0.45, 0.30, 0.22, 0.15 m; steepness at most 0.034), which carry all height above 25 m; the FFT heights are 0.11 m rms. The long-wave layer added after the review is normals only (no parallax, no occlusion, no silhouette) and is drawn as two copies of one realisation rotated by -20 and +20 deg (`longWaveSpreadDeg` 20; `surfaceWgsl.ts` lines 347-366), which widens its spread. The cascades below 25 m have broad spreading by nature.
- **Fix direction:** Get `ocean-5` answered by the lead (`docs/requests/ocean-5.md` is "Status: declined": core-support declined it as not its path and escalated it): at least 8 swell components (`MAX_SWELL`) with non-commensurate wavelengths around the 70 m peak, a directional spread of about +-30 deg and realistic steepness, or a low-resolution spectral height field for 25-200 m that physics samples too. In the ocean's own paths: keep the long-wave layer's own directional spectrum (two independent realisations instead of rotated copies, or a rotation of a few degrees) and raise its weight against the short chop in the shading. If the lead agrees, draw the long-wave layer's height too, blended to pure swell within about 10 hull radii of any vessel so the 5 cm waterline criterion still holds where it matters, and report it with the FFT term. Compare 150 m, 500 m and 3 km shots with aerial photographs for a readable wind direction.

- **Real reference:** ISS, Gemini and Apollo sunglint photographs. A silver-white to golden core, often saturated at the specular point, with a filigree inside it: long curved slick lines, packets of internal waves (parallel arcs 0.5-2 km apart), ship and island wakes many kilometres long, calm tongues and wind shadows in the lee of land, eddies traced by slicks, cloud shadows. There are no line features, no flow-like warping and no dependence on coast or islands; from orbit only four octaves remain. With 7 m/s everywhere the peak radiance is about 4.5 kcd/m2, mid-grey at the lab's EV 15 (`labs/ocean/main.ts` line 50) and a fifth of a sunlit cloud.
- **Fix direction:** Let the slope variance vary over at least a decade across the visible disc (calm pools to trade-wind seas) while the area mean stays at Cox-Munk, so the width check still holds. Warp the slick coordinates with a curl-noise flow so they form eddies and fronts, use ridged noise for hard-edged filament lines and wind-aligned streaks from 1 to 50 km, add internal-wave packets as sets of parallel arcs in a few places, raise the calm contrast (variance x 0.2-0.3 in slicks), and add lee calm zones from the terrain's land mask if the core can supply one. Expose the lab view by meter, compare side by side with two or three ISS glint frames, and verify in the integrated app that the core of the glint clips and feeds bloom.

## 7. Seen from above, whitecaps are a star field of specks and round blobs

- **Severity:** major (photo: major, artifact: major). Merged from photo 4 and artifact 5.
- **Screenshots:** `foam-coverage-debug.png`, `foam-coverage-150m.png`, `alt500.png`, `climb-273m.png`, `handover-stray-water.png`, `perf-coast-1440p.png`
- **Checked:** visible in all six. `foam-coverage-debug.png` looks like a night sky with two globular clusters at (490, 448) and (806, 18). In the shaded views the whitecaps are single-pixel specks and a few round blobs 2-3 m across, with no common orientation.
- **Defect:** Whitecaps are hundreds of isolated 1-2 px white pinpoints plus a few round "popcorn" blobs, uniformly sprinkled like salt, with no shape, no direction and no trails. The mean coverage is calibrated (0.117 %); the size and shape distribution is not.
- **Real reference:** At 7 m/s (Beaufort 4, "fairly frequent white horses") whitecaps seen from 100-500 m are crescents and bars along breaking crests, 2-10 m long and 0.5-2 m wide, all lying across the wind, each leaving a fading foam patch and a streak downwind, often lined up in windrows. A frame holds tens of distinct elongated strokes at different ages, not hundreds of dots.
- **Likely cause:** `fftWgsl.ts` line 187: fresh foam is bred per cascade where that cascade's own Jacobian falls `foamSigma` = 3.0 standard deviations below 1 (`foamRampSigma` 0.8). Excursions of a near-isotropic Gaussian field at 3 sigma are compact, near-circular maxima, so only isolated extreme-tail texels fire. Breaking is not tied to the dominant waves (the swell and the long-wave layer never contribute). Line 188 lets foam decay where it was born, at 0.6 per second, with no advection and no stretching. Where the cascades are filtered the statistical coverage arrives as isolated pixels (`surfaceWgsl.ts` line 322).
- **Fix direction:** Compute breaking from the combined surface: the sum of the cascades' convergence plus the local steepness and crest proximity of the swell and the long-wave layer, thresholded lower (about 1.5-2 sigma) after a low-pass along the crest direction, so whole crest segments fire as strips. Advect the foam with the crest and stretch it downwind. Decay in two stages: bright active foam, then a residual streak that lives 5-10 s. Re-calibrate the mean to Monahan afterwards and add a spec on the patch-size distribution (for example median connected foam area above 3 m2 from 150 m).
