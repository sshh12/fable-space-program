# vehicle-pad: merged visual QA work list (wave 2)

**Verdict.** Not ready: photo lens 4 (103 screenshots), artifact lens 4 (97 screenshots). The geometry is there: the outer walls of the engine bells, the tower lattice, the interstage interior and the landing gear carry real detail, and the one orbit frame from the integrated game (`out/screens/integration/budget-leo.png`) reads well at distance. The package fails where a player looks hardest. Under the real terrain the launch complex hovers over the ground on a hard-edged circular disc; the capsule reads as a tiled kiln; the lander's foil is black-and-white static with a cracked-mud cell pattern; frost on the tanks is hard-edged stippled cut-outs; the launch mount is dressed with cones, slabs and a ribbon stair. Behind those: a standing dither stipple in every engine view, stages that are plain tubes, a single flat colour for the entry glow, char and soot that do not look burnt, night lighting from unshadowed point lights, thin structures and their shadows that break into dots, and markings chopped up by the stringers they cross. All acceptance pictures were shot in a lab without terrain or atmosphere, about a stop under-exposed and with the renderer's noise frozen, and the lander was never pictured touching the ground. This list merges 37 critic findings (20 photo, 17 artifact) into 25 items: 5 blockers, 14 major, 6 minor, ordered by how much a player would notice (blockers, then majors, then minors; the most visible first inside each band). Where the lenses rate a defect differently the item carries the higher rating and names both. I opened 74 screenshots (every one the critics cite) and 30 zoomed crops: every item is visible in the cited pictures and none was dropped. Where a critic's wording goes further than the picture, or a claim cannot be settled from a still, the item's "Checked" line says so.

The last vehicle-pad commit is 22786a1 (2026-11-10 21:38) and there are no uncommitted changes under `src/render/vehicle`, `labs/vehicle`, `tests/browser/vehicle`, `tools/vehicle` or the site and visual defs, so the spec views show the current code. Work that serves several items:
- **Find out what a player sees before judging the dots (items 4, 6, 14, 15).** `settle()` in `tests/browser/vehicle/lab.ts:14-17` switches the renderer to deterministic mode, which pins the blue-noise offset to [0, 0] (`src/render/core/renderer.ts:527` and `:618`). Every dithered blend (LOD cross-fade, the frost shell's coverage, the cascade transition, the shadow penumbra) is therefore frozen into the stills as dots, and 48 settled frames average nothing. Live, the same pixels are a blend that temporal anti-aliasing has to average; whether that resolves or shimmers is not established. Capture the engine, apron-wall, tower and frost views live, static and with a slow camera move, first.

- **Texture re-bakes in `tools/vehicle/bake-textures.ts`:** tile (2), foil (3), foam (7), ablator and a char set (10), soot streak mask (12), brushed steel (18).
- **Re-shoot under the real sky, terrain and exposure (item 25).** Until then the material judgements in items 2, 3, 7, 12 and 20 are provisional.

## 1. Under the real terrain the launch complex does not sit on the ground

- **Severity:** blocker (photo)
- **Screenshots:** `out/screens/integration/budget-pad-lift-off.png` (2560 x 1440: tank farm x 1400-1800, y 740-940; crawlerway x 2040-2400, y 820-980; pad disc in the lower left), `out/screens/integration/shadows-pad-with-the-site-and-the-vehicle-low-sun.png` (1280 x 720: pad disc x 220-1050, y 320-410; far tank farm and pipe run x 0-560, y 270-330).
- **Checked:** visible in both. In the lift-off frame the tank-farm slab is a thin plate with a dark underside edge along both near sides, standing clear of the slope, and its pipe run leaves the near corner and slants down through the air toward the pad. The crawlerway is a paper-thin strip laid over the terrain; its near edge shows as a pale line, not the dark underside the critic describes. The pad stands on a flat grey disc with a hard, exactly circular rim in both frames. The size of the gaps cannot be measured from the stills.
- **Defect:** The complex is placed on one plane and the land falls away under it. The tank-farm slab hovers above the slope with its pipe run leaving the corner as a bridge in mid-air, the crawlerway is a razor-thin strip floating over the terrain, and the whole pad sits on a hard-edged, perfectly circular grey disc. None of the package's own 97 screenshots can show this: the vehicle lab puts the site on a relief-free sphere (`padElevation: () => 0`), and the hand-off lists a terrain-derived elevation as untested.
- **Real reference:** A real complex is graded into the land: the pad is a raised mound or cut with sloped embankments, tank farms sit on bermed, levelled pads with retaining walls, pipe lines run on stanchions of varying height that follow the ground, crawlerways are a river-rock roadbed on an embankment, and every edge meets soil with a shoulder, a drainage ditch and tyre-worn dirt. No slab ever shows its underside.
- **Likely cause:** `src/render/vehicle/siteFeature.ts:86-90` (`elevationOf`) resolves one `padElevation` for the whole site and every structure is placed on that plane, while the site reaches 330 m and more from the pad (crawlerway length 330 m, pipe runs 256 and 278 m, two tank farms in `data/defs/sites/ka-lae-lc1.structures.json`). The terrain feature paints and flattens only a disc of `Math.min(params.apronRadiusM, site.pad.flatRadius)` (`src/render/terrain/feature.ts:313-319`), although `data/defs/sites/ka-lae-lc1.json` line 7 says flatRadius 450 / blendRadius 900. Slabs are thin (apron thickness 0.3 m in the structures file) and footings reach well under a metre below the pad plane, so any drop in the terrain exposes an underside.
- **Fix direction:** Give `createSiteFeature` a per-structure ground-height callback (east-north-up offset from the pad to terrain height) and place each structure at its own ground height. Build every slab, kerb and footing with a skirt or embankment that goes several metres below grade. Stand pipe runs on stanchions whose length follows the terrain and drape the crawlerway over it (or carry it on a graded embankment mesh). In parallel file a terrain request so the flattening and blend really cover the site footprint and the pad disc gets a graded, irregular, soil-blended edge instead of a circle. Add an integration screenshot from a low side angle of the tank farm and crawlerway, and an assertion on the largest slab-to-terrain gap.

## 2. The capsule reads as a tiled kiln

- **Severity:** blocker (photo blocker, artifact major)
- **Screenshots:** `stage-capsule-space.png`, `capsule-windows.png`, `capsule-hatch.png`, `capsule-charred-back-shell.png`, `stage-lander-space.png`, `stage-ascent-space.png`, `staging-05-m-3-separation.png` to `staging-10-mains.png`.
- **Checked:** visible in all. Cream square tiles with dark joints and about one tan tile in eight cover the cone and the apex tunnel, which ends in a rolled lip with no hardware on it; the pattern is still readable in the staging frames. Both windows in `capsule-windows.png` sit in a rectangle of tiles laid at another angle that slices the course tiles along its border (x 380-590 and x 690-900, y 230-510). The KESTREL marking is a plain pale rectangle. The strip down the cone in `stage-capsule-space.png` (x 660-740, y 205-600) shows a sawtooth highlight like a twisted drill bit.
- **Defect:** The hero object of the game reads as masonry: a cone and a cylindrical "chimney" covered in tiles about 0.2 m across with wide dark grout lines and a checkerboard of tan tiles, topped by a rolled lip. There is no sheen, no panel structure, no forward-bay or parachute-cover geometry, and the apex tunnel is tiled like a chimney pot with no docking hardware. Round every window, the hatch and the marking there is a rectangular patch whose tile grid is laid at a different angle and offset, so tiles are sliced mid-way along the patch border. The marking wipes the tiles out like a sticker. The "tape" strips down the cone carry the quilt normal map.
- **Real reference:** Apollo command module: mirror-like aluminised Kapton tape in long strips, with wrinkles and seams, reflecting the Earth and black sky. Orion: black back-shell tiles under silver aluminised tape, a few large trapezoidal panels, sharp panel breaks, a forward bay cover with gores. Dragon: smooth white panels with hairline seams. In all of them tile gaps are about 1 mm, filled flush and invisible beyond a metre, tone varies by 2-3 % at most, and the top is a docking tunnel with a ring, latches and a hatch. Close-out tiles round a window are cut to fit and their outline follows tile edges. Markings are painted across tiles with the joints showing through. Joint tape is a smooth reflective strip.
- **Likely cause:** `tools/vehicle/bake-textures.ts` `tile()` (from line 283): the gap is written into the albedo at 0.36 (line 346) over a half-width of 0.007-0.016 tile, which is a 2.5 to 6 mm dark joint on a 180 mm tile that survives mip-mapping; 12 % "toasted" tiles (`kind > 0.88`, line 338: shade -0.07, warm 0.04) plus 7 % bright replacements. `public/assets/vehicle/tile_albedo.png` and `tile_n.png` hold 64 tiles on a 1.44 m repeat. Material `tile-white` (`src/render/vehicle/materials.ts:77`) is a plain rough dielectric. Tile size 0.18 m and the courses are set in `src/render/vehicle/parts/capsule.ts:168-175`; the tunnel and its lip use the same shell material. Close-outs: `capsule.ts:280-325` gives the rectangular grid block round each opening planar UVs square to the opening (`planarUv`). Marking: an opaque decal rectangle (`capsule.ts:534`). Tape: `capsule.ts:598-605` builds 35 mm boxes in `blanket-silver`, which uses `quilt_n` on a 0.8 m repeat.
- **Fix direction:** Pick one real back-shell vocabulary and build it: either aluminised-tape strips (metallic 1, roughness about 0.25, anisotropic along the strip, strip seams and wrinkles in the normal map) or large white or black panels 0.4 to 0.8 m across. In either case re-bake the tile set with joints of 1-2 mm equivalent carried by the normal, occlusion and roughness maps only (albedo contrast under 10 %, fading with mip level), cut tile-to-tile tone variation to 2-3 % and drop the tan checker. Keep the course UVs through the opening blocks and re-lay only the ring of close-out tiles next to the frame, ending on course joints. Composite markings over the tile texture (bake the marking into a tile-backed atlas region, or multiply the tile joints into the decal). Give the tape its own smooth aluminised material. Model the forward bay: parachute compartment gores, apex cover seam, docking ring with petals and latches, tunnel hatch; give the shoulder a visible seal band and the thruster ports scarfed recesses instead of black blocks.

## 3. The lander's insulation does not read as foil

- **Severity:** blocker (photo blocker, artifact major)
- **Screenshots:** `lander-deployed.png`, `lander-stowed.png`, `lander-leg-intact.png`, `lander-mli-closeup.png`, `stage-lander-space.png`, `stage-ascent-space.png`.
- **Checked:** visible in all six. In `lander-deployed.png` the silver panel (x 560-720, y 160-330) holds pixel values from 0 to 255 side by side. `lander-mli-closeup.png` is a field of flat polygonal cells with bright ridges. In `stage-ascent-space.png` the highlight stops dead on a vertical seam at x 650: bright on the left panel, dark on the right. The gold lobes are exact cylinders with one dark seam line.
- **Defect:** (a) From 10-15 m under a low sun, which is the landing view, the silver blankets are a black-and-white camouflage mosaic and the gold lobes are blotchy brass or gold leaf. (b) Close up the crinkle is an obvious Voronoi "cracked mud" pattern of flat cells with raised ridges, identical on gold, silver and black; it looks like hammered brass or reptile skin. (c) In orbit light the silver blanket shows cloudy noise and the highlight is cut at a panel seam. (d) The film is stretched tight over mathematically perfect cylinders with flat matte tops and a single dark line for a seam: no tape, tie-downs, overlap or sag. The hand-off calls this "noisy"; a player would call it a broken texture.
- **Real reference:** Insulation on the Apollo lunar module and on current landers is a loose, baggy film: pillows and creases 10 to 40 cm across, long soft wrinkles radiating from fasteners, taped overlapping panels with visible tape seams, grommets and stand-off buttons, sagging between fasteners so the silhouette is never a clean cylinder. Each crease is a long, sharp, mirror-bright glint; between glints the film is a smooth dark or amber mirror of the sky and the ground, not pixel-scale speckle. A panel stays recognisably gold or silver across its area. Gold is amber Kapton with a deep orange cast in the folds; black Kapton and bare aluminised panels sit beside it.
- **Likely cause:** `tools/vehicle/bake-textures.ts` `mli()` (from line 369): the normal map `public/assets/vehicle/mli_n.png` (1024 px on a 1.1 m repeat) is two Worley cell layers (20 x 34 and 8 x 13 cells), each cell a flat tilted facet, so the highlight is quantised per cell. With metallic 0.85-0.9, roughness 0.38-0.4 and anisotropy -0.6 (`mli-gold`, `mli-silver`, `src/render/vehicle/materials.ts:79-80`) every 4-10 cm facet flips between sun, sky and black, and nothing widens the lobe as the cells shrink below a pixel. The hull under it is an exact lathe (`src/render/vehicle/parts/lander.ts`), so there is no geometric sag, overlap or panel edge. The seam step is the anisotropy tangent (or the UV) flipping between adjacent panel strips.
- **Fix direction:** Replace the cell facets by a crease-based height field (ridged, directional folds at 5-40 cm plus a fine crinkle at lower amplitude), baked with a roughness map that is low on the film and higher on tape; raise the base roughness toward 0.5 with variation. Add real geometry at LOD0: blanket panels as separately offset, slightly pillowed patches with overlapping edges, tape strips, grommets and buttons, and 1-3 cm of low-frequency displacement so silhouettes wobble. Bake normal variance into the roughness mips (Toksvig or LEAN, beyond the mesh shader's Kaplanyan term) so at 10 m and more the blanket converges to a broad streaky sheen instead of speckle. Make tangents continuous round each body. Re-shoot at sun elevations of 5, 16 and 40 degrees.

## 4. Frost on the tanks is hard-edged cut-out patches filled with stipple

- **Severity:** blocker (artifact blocker, photo minor)
- **Screenshots:** `frost-prelaunch.png`, `frost-line-prelaunch.png`, `pad-stack-tower-15m.png`, `pad-arms-retracted.png`; for comparison without frost `frost-shed.png`, `frost-line-shed.png`.
- **Checked:** visible in all four. In `frost-prelaunch.png` pale rounded patches with straight edges cover the tank and the MERIDIAN lettering and roundel are grey ghosts (they are crisp blue in `frost-shed.png`). In `frost-line-prelaunch.png` the LOX stencil and the CAUTION CRYOGENIC placard are barely legible, and a row of white specks runs along the lower ring of the corrugated band (x 400-860, y 405-415); the row is absent in `frost-line-shed.png`. From the pad camera (`pad-arms-retracted.png`) the tank lettering is a column of grey dots. No vapour anywhere. How much of the stipple survives live is open (see the first shared note); the hard patch outlines do not depend on it.
- **Defect:** On the pad the frosting tanks are covered with flat, hard-edged patches (rounded rectangles and wedges on the 0.2 x 0.5 m skin grid) filled with a fixed screen-door stipple. It reads as peeling masking paper or a thin coat of whitewash, not frost: almost no brightness or texture change on the white paint, no build-up toward the tank bottom, no vapour, and the markings under it become ghosts. White specks poke out along the corrugated intertank band where the shell cuts through the corrugation valleys. It is on the vehicle in the first view of the game.
- **Real reference:** Frost on a loaded oxygen tank is a continuous matte white coat over the whole cryogenic barrel between the frost lines, visibly brighter than the paint, with soft vertical streaks, thicker rime low on the barrel and on weld lands and stiffener rings, bare streaks where ice has slid off, sheets peeling at lift-off and a curtain of condensation fog rolling down the skin. Markings under thin frost keep sharp edges and lose contrast. There are no polygon outlines and no dot texture. The two critics describe the frost line differently (crisp at the dome welds; feathered): it is straight and follows the weld at vehicle scale and feathers over a few decimetres close up.
- **Likely cause:** `src/render/vehicle/parts/stage.ts:356-429`: the rime is a separate shell cut from `frostPatch()` (line 87) on the lathe grid, hence the polygon edges, and emitted with `fade: cover * k` where `rimeCover` is 0.72 (lines 424-429), which is permanently dithered coverage in the mesh shader (`src/render/core/mesh/meshSystem.ts:130-134`). Even resolved it is a 72 % blend of a hard-edged shell. The shell stands a few millimetres proud, less than the corrugation amplitude at the intertank. Material `frost` is albedo 0.9-0.97 over paint at 0.8 (`src/render/vehicle/materials.ts:86` and `:44`), so the brightness step is small.
- **Fix direction:** Remove the dithered shell. Put frost into surface shading: a frost weight per vertex times a texture-space noise threshold (centimetre-scale noise, streaked along the axis, soft smoothstep edge) that drives albedo to white, roughness to 0.8 and above, the foam normal map in, and the clear coat to 0. The last needs the core mesh shader's frost term to scale the clear coat in `gbufferExtra` (file a render-core request; the hand-off notes the term cannot do it today). Raise the albedo contrast against the paint and add fine sparkle, thicken the frost toward the aft dome and below fittings, and keep the frost line as a straight band feathered over 0.1-0.3 m. If any shell stays, give it fade 1 and stop it short of the corrugated bands. Hand the effects package anchor points for vent fog and falling ice.

## 5. The launch mount is dressed with primitives

- **Severity:** blocker (artifact blocker, photo major)
- **Screenshots:** `pad-engine-section.png`, `pad-hold-down-clamp.png`, `site-apron.png`, `pad-stack-300m.png`, `pad-stack-tower-15m.png`, `engines-clean.png`.
- **Checked:** visible in all. Rainbirds are faceted red cones on grey posts (one fills the foreground of `pad-hold-down-clamp.png`, x 580-660, y 510-720). Clamps are flat white pylons with one pin, a yellow arm with a row of grey squares and triangular gussets that render as stipple. The deck is a flat blue-grey plane with a yellow ring and a plain round hole. The stair on the mount in `site-apron.png` (x 740-800, y 140-330) is a black zig-zag. The swing-arm plate in `pad-stack-tower-15m.png` ends in three bent pipe stubs with air between them and the vehicle. Walkway undersides are black (item 17).
- **Defect:** The pad deck, the closest thing a player sees before launch, is a stage set. Rainbirds are red cones on grey poles (toy megaphones). Hold-down clamps are flat extruded "tombstones" with a yellow chequered tip that merely touch the skirt. The tail service mast is a plain grey box on a slab post with three bent pipes ending on a bare plate. The stair to the deck is a black zig-zag ribbon of flat plates with no treads, landings or rails. The mount is a concrete box on four square piers and its deck is an untextured plane. Swing arms end in a block with pipe stubs that connect to nothing: no hoses, cables, purge ducts, drip trays or vapour. Next to engine bells that do carry detail these read as placeholder art at a glance, and they are in the first frame of every launch. The hand-off itself names clamps, rainbirds and missing hoses as weak.
- **Real reference:** Mounts at LC-39, SLC-41, Starbase and the SLS and Saturn pads: rainbirds are large galvanised or oxide-red goose-neck pipes 0.6-1 m in diameter with flanges, bolt circles, flattened fan nozzles and support struts; hold-down arms are massive cast or welded links with pins, hydraulic cylinders, blast shrouds and bolted bases, scorched; service masts have hoods, hinge frames and bundles of flex hoses; the deck is steel plate and refractory concrete with seams, bolt heads, drain slots, cable trays, rust runs, scorch fans and standing water; stairs have stringers, open-grate treads and yellow rails; umbilicals carry flex hoses, cable looms and frosted cryogenic lines with venting vapour.
- **Likely cause:** `src/render/vehicle/site/pad.ts:367-376` (a rainbird is a rod plus a frustum in flat red paint), the launch-mount block at `pad.ts:280-366` (box girder, piers), `pad.ts:399` (the stair is a beam plate 1.1 m by 0.06 m), the deck as one flat surface of `concrete`, whose 18 m repeat has no fine grain (`materials.ts:96`); `padClamp` in `src/render/vehicle/parts/structures.ts:339`; swing arms in `src/render/vehicle/site/tower.ts` carry lines but nothing spans the last metre to the vehicle. The complex has 160 k triangles at LOD0 against 1.1 M for the vehicle; none of it is spent here.
- **Fix direction:** Model these as hardware. Rainbird: riser pipe with flange, elbow, flared fan head and support struts in galvanised steel. Clamp: boxed weldment with ribs, hinge pin and lugs, hydraulic actuator, shroud and blast shield, an arm with a pad that visibly bears on hard points of the skirt, proper diagonal hazard stripes from the decal sheet, a bolted base plate. Service mast: hood, hinge and a hose bundle to the vehicle's umbilical plate. Stair: stringers, grating treads, landings and rails. Deck: plates, hatches, refractory pads, drain grates, scorch and rust masks, a fine-grain detail normal. Hang catenary hose and cable bundles from each umbilical plate to the vehicle's plates (they retract with the arm). Drop the dithered gussets (item 6).

## 6. Dither stipple: parts near a LOD switch are drawn as dots

- **Severity:** major (both lenses)
- **Screenshots:** `engines-clean.png`, `engines-sooted.png`, `stage-m1-aft-space.png`, `stage-m2-aft-space.png`, `stage-m2-space.png`, `stage-m1-space.png`, `pad-engine-section.png`, `pad-hold-down-clamp.png`, `pad-arms-retracted.png`, `site-crew-arm-from-tower.png`; also `out/screens/integration/budget-pad-lift-off.png`.
- **Checked:** visible in all. Every bell extension and lip in the four engine views is a ring of dots with the nozzle interior or the sky showing through, and the sooted bells sparkle white along the rim. The interstage-2 cone on top of M-2 in `stage-m2-space.png` (x 560-720, y 30-100) is salt-and-pepper with a garbled MERIDIAN decal and a black rectangle beside it. Clamp gussets are stipple. In the integrated lift-off frame the bracing of the near lightning mast is dashed (x 1260-1480, y 960-1260 of the 2560 px frame); I do not know whether that run captured with the noise frozen. Whether a player sees shimmer cannot be told from stills. The dotted shadows the photo critic lists here are under item 14, the dotted access-door outlines under item 16.
- **Defect:** Any part whose projected size is within 15 % of a LOD switch is drawn as two complementary stipple patterns for as long as the camera stays there. Nozzle extensions look perforated, the interstage cone at 40 m is salt-and-pepper with a doubled decal, the tower lattice sparkles. It is in at least ten acceptance views, including every engine view, so those views cannot be judged as they stand.
- **Real reference:** Real hardware has continuous surfaces. A LOD change must not be visible at all: a shipped renderer swaps within a frame or two behind temporal anti-aliasing or cross-fades for a fraction of a second after the switch. No surface is left half-transparent because of the distance the camera happens to be at.
- **Likely cause:** `src/render/core/mesh/meshSystem.ts:20` and `:649-660`: `LOD_FADE_BAND = 0.15` is a band in projected size, so a camera parked in the band keeps both LODs dithered indefinitely (blue-noise discard at lines 130-134). The stills freeze the noise (first shared note). Package side: engine `lodPixels` [200, 44] (`src/render/vehicle/parts/engine.ts:381`) and the default [220, 60] on stage structures put the switch exactly at the size these parts have in the standard 9 to 40 m views, and LOD1 differs from LOD0 in surface, bands and decal regions, so even a resolved blend ghosts.
- **Fix direction:** First establish the truth with live captures, static and in a slow dolly through the switch distance, and make the acceptance capture show what a player sees (keep the noise sequence running while settling, or accumulate a full noise cycle). Package: lower the first switch for hero parts (engines to about 90-110 px, interstages and skirts to about 120 px) so the 5-150 m hero distances are not near a threshold, and make LOD1 keep the same surface, silhouette, material bands and decal regions as LOD0 so a blend is invisible. Core request: replace the size band by a time-based cross-fade with hysteresis (about 0.25-0.3 s after the switch, then exactly one LOD). Then re-shoot these views.

## 7. Seen alone, the stages are smooth tubes with a texture

- **Severity:** major (both lenses)
- **Screenshots:** `stage-m1-space.png`, `stage-m2-space.png`, `stage-m3-space.png`, `stage-ascent-space.png`, `stage-m2-aft-space.png`, `interstage-interior.png`, `closeup-foam.png`, `staging-00-stack.png` to `staging-04-m-2-separation.png`, `pad-stack-300m.png`.
- **Checked:** visible in all. M-1 is a clean white tube with one black band; M-2 a flat orange cylinder with about ten soft smudges at near-regular spacing; M-3 a cream cylinder whose bump reads as leather above a chrome band; the ascent stage a foil drum with a blank pale rectangle for a radiator (x 550-650, y 410-555). Slightly less absolute than written: `closeup-foam.png` does show vertical aged streaks close up, and M-1 carries one raceway on its right edge; neither reads from 40 m.
- **Defect:** From 40 m nothing breaks the silhouette on the visible side and there is no weathering, staining or stencilling at readable scale. M-2's smudges are the 9.6 m foam tile repeating. Under M-2 the inside of the aft skirt is a flat, untextured primer-green band with no frames or stringers, and the thrust-structure blanket is a perfectly regular diamond lattice. The M-2 dome inside the interstage is a bare disc with three thin pipes. Close up the foam is an even popcorn bump that turns to noise at grazing angles.

## 18. Brushed steel shows scan-line banding

- **Severity:** major (artifact)
- **Screenshots:** `closeup-brushed-steel.png`, `stage-m3-space.png`, `stage-m1-space.png`.
- **Checked:** visible in all three. The highlight on the interstage in the close-up (x 690-790, y 50-610) is built from horizontal stripes with stepped ends; on M-3's lower steel section (x 590-680, y 500-660) faint regular horizontal lines cross the streak. The box fairing in the close-up (x 990-1120, y 385-515) stands in dark speckle and has one bright white face. I confirmed that `public/assets/vehicle/brushed_n.png` holds 2 distinct red and 4 distinct green values.
- **Defect:** The anisotropic highlight is made of blocky rows that repeat down the surface instead of a fine grain. The box fairing on the hull sits in blotchy speckle (unresolved shadow or occlusion noise round its base) with one face rendered as a bright strip.
- **Real reference:** Brushed stainless has a continuous, fine, slightly irregular grain: the highlight is a soft streak whose edges feather into countless hair-thin scratches, with no visible rows, steps or repeat, plus faint handling marks and weld tint.
- **Likely cause:** `tools/vehicle/bake-textures.ts:48-63`: the brushed height field is 0.00003 m, so `brushed_n.png` is flat, and all visible grain comes from the roughness in `brushed_orm`, which is constant along u ("each scratch is a line of constant v"): perfectly straight 1.5 mm rows on a 0.4 m repeat, magnified by the anisotropic lobe (`materials.ts:54-57`).

Check the speckle round the box fairing again after the live capture of item 14.
