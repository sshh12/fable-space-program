# atmosphere-clouds: merged visual QA work list (wave 2)

**Verdict.** Photo lens 4 / 10 (83 screenshots), artifact lens 4 / 10 (82 screenshots). The clear air is credible (sky gradients, the limb from orbit, the Belt of Venus and the Earth's shadow band), but the clouds do not pass as clouds at any distance, and they are in almost every view of a mission. From the pad, fair-weather cumulus is a ceiling of smooth grey putty under a milky veil. From orbit the cloud layer is a blurred overlay of equal-sized blobs that casts no shadows. At low sun the cloud-shadow map shows through as saw teeth, straight stripes and combs. Everything a cloud shades turns navy instead of grey, overcast decks are covered in contour lines, and far cloud banks are built from horizontal bars. This list merges the 31 findings of the two lenses (16 photo, 15 artifact) into 21 items: 3 blocker, 12 major, 6 minor. I opened the cited screenshots for every item (full frames, enlarged crops, and pixel measurements where a number was claimed). Every defect is visible, and no item was dropped. Three kinds of claim could not be checked from the pictures and are marked where they occur: differences between two runs of the suite (re-running it would rewrite the screenshots), statements about the hand-off document, and the causes in code (the identifiers, line numbers and tunable values exist as cited; the causal chains are the critics' reading and were not traced at run time).

**How to read this list.** Items are ordered by how much a player would notice, within blocker, major and minor. Where the lenses rated one defect differently, the item carries the higher rating and names both. "Checked" says what I saw when I opened the screenshots.

- All screenshots are in `C:\dev\fable-space-game\out\screens\atmosphere\`. Each item gives the full path of its main screenshot and file names in that folder for the rest.
- Pixel coordinates refer to the 1280 x 720 frame unless marked "native" (the 2560 x 1440 perf frames).
- "shaders/clouds.ts" is `src\render\atmosphere\shaders\clouds.ts`, "shaders/march.ts" is `src\render\atmosphere\shaders\march.ts`, and "clouds.ts" alone is `src\render\atmosphere\clouds.ts`. Tunables are in `data\defs\render\earth.clouds.json`.
- The eight `perf-*-1440p.png` frames are about ten minutes older than the last commit to the package (item 21). They are cited because they are the only native 1440p frames in animated mode. Items say where a defect is visible only in one of them.
- The last two sections group items that share a cause and map every source finding to its item.

## Blocker

### 1. Fair-weather cumulus from the pad is smooth grey putty under a milky veil

- **Severity:** blocker (photo: blocker, artifact: blocker)
- **Screenshots:** `C:\dev\fable-space-game\out\screens\atmosphere\clouds-ground-cumulus.png` (flutes x 60-540, y 120-380; grey gaps x 900-1100, y 80-260). Same folder: `clouds-static-animated.png`, `clouds-quality-0.7-ground.png`, `clouds-ground-cumulus-into-sun.png`, `perf-ground-cumulus-overhead-1440p.png`, `perf-pad-1440p.png` (clouds x 200-1000, y 300-750 native; fan x 800-1100, y 380-760 native), `transparent-quads-broken-layer.png`, `clouds-ascent-2kms-1800m-moving.png`, `clouds-ascent-2kms-1800m-converged.png`.
- **Defect:** The "fair-weather cumulus at noon" view is a ceiling of huge smooth grey lumps that reads as putty or a smoke simulation.
  1. Bodies overhead span half the frame and have no cauliflower detail at any scale. Nothing is finer than a few hundred metres, and the edges are a soft ramp 2 to 4 px wide, so the clouds look out of focus against the sharp terrain.
  2. The undersides are fluted: long smeared streaks along the view rays, like pulled taffy.
  3. There are no flat dark bases and no bright sunlit tops. Underside and flank are the same light grey.
  4. The gaps between clouds are neutral grey, not blue, because a translucent sheet lies over the field. The map's cover is a uniform 0.4, yet about 2 % of the sky pixels are blue.
  5. Seen from below against clear sky, one cumulus is a uniform grey blob with a blurred outline.
  6. Flying past clouds at 1.8 km, the frame is a featureless grey gradient with nothing to focus on.
  7. In animated mode at native 1440p the clouds are airbrushed against razor-sharp terrain, and a featureless translucent fan ends in a curved rim across clear blue sky.
- **Checked:** Confirmed. In `clouds-ground-cumulus.png` the gaps measure (149, 148, 150) at (520, 300) and (153, 151, 152) at (1050, 200). Above the horizon (rows 0 to 440), 1.9 % of pixels have B - R > 18 and 0.7 % have B - R > 28; `clouds-static-animated.png` and `clouds-quality-0.7-ground.png` give 1.6 % and 1.5 %. The flutes, the missing dark bases and the soft edges are plain in the full frame. `transparent-quads-broken-layer.png` shows the single grey blob, the two 1800 m frames show the grey gradient, and a native crop of `perf-pad-1440p.png` shows the fan with its rim. The fan is visible only in that perf frame.
- **Real reference:** Launch-pad and airfield photographs under fair-weather cumulus: separate clouds 0.3 to 1.5 km across, flat grey bases all at one height and two to three stops darker than the tops, brilliant white cauliflower tops whose curled edges are sharp down to the pixel, ragged wisps at the fringes, saturated blue between them, and ranks of clouds foreshortened toward the horizon. A cover of 0.4 means more blue than white overhead. Next to a cumulus you see turbulent knots and filaments metres across.
- **Likely cause:**
  - Shape: `cloudDensityAt` in shaders/clouds.ts (from l.154; the threshold is near l.181-227) thresholds one Perlin-Worley lookup of a 128^3 texture tiled over `baseNoiseScaleM` = 9000 m, times a height gradient. A texel is 70 m and the dominant features are about 2.2 km; the octaves halve in amplitude, so 140 m detail carries about 3 % of the signal. The low layer is 1.9 km thick, so the shapes are near-vertical extrusions of 2 km blobs.
  - Blur and flutes, two candidates: the noise is read one mip above the footprint and `footprint = max(pixel, 0.25 * ruleDt)` ties that mip to the step length (`stepMaxM` 600), which blurs along the ray (artifact lens); `windShear` 0.6 (`cloudWind`, l.169) shears the lookup about 1.1 km over the layer depth (photo lens). Setting `windShear` to 0 and taking the footprint from the pixel alone will tell which one dominates.
  - Edges: `lowEdgeSoftness` 0.12, and an erosion that only displaces the surface by at most `detailErosion` 0.24 from a 32^3 detail texture (34 m per texel) and is gone by `detailFadeFootprintM` 400. That gives no crisp edge and no holes.
  - Veil: `cloudColumnAt` (near l.131) gives every column with coverage above `highCoverageStart` 0.22 a stratiform sheet under the map's cloud top, even when that top (3 km in this scene) is at cumulus height.
  - Cover: the threshold is lowered by half the erosion depth (`thr = 1 - cLow - 0.5 k`, l.196), by `fairWeatherCoverage`, and by the mesoscale swing `mesoscaleAmplitude` 0.8 x 4 c (1 - c) (l.125).
  - Lighting: the two-stream `highOrders` term and the ground and sky ambient make the base as bright as the flank.
  - Buffer: half resolution, one ray per 2 x 2 block per frame, plain bilinear upsample (`textureSampleLevel(cloudTex, linearClamp, uvCloud)`).
- **Fix direction:**
  - Rebuild the low-cloud shape the way shipped renderers that pass this test do (Schneider / Horizon, Hillaire / Frostbite): a cumulus-scale base noise (tile 3 to 4 km, vertical frequency at least the horizontal one), density = remap(base, 1 - coverage, 1, 0, 1) times a height profile per cloud type, then erosion by three octaves of high-frequency Worley fBm (a 64^3 or larger detail texture) weighted to the edges and deep enough to open holes, curl-noise distortion of the detail lookup, an edge softness an order of magnitude smaller, and a wind shear of at most 0.15.
  - Take the noise mip from the pixel footprint only. Handle long steps with more steps inside resolved cloud, not with blur.
  - Put no sheet where the map's cloud top lies within about 1.5 km of the low layer's top; give that coverage to the cumulus. Where a sheet exists, make it white and fibrous.
  - Darken the bases: attenuate sun and sky by the optical depth above the sample, and drop the ground bounce to a physically small value.
  - Keep the detail through the resolve: an edge-aware upsample guided by alpha and depth, or a full-resolution march near the camera, as is done for small shells.
  - Add a spec that the sky fraction seen from the ground equals the map's cover (0.4 within 0.1). Iterate at native 1440p in animated mode beside pad photographs.

### 2. From low orbit the clouds are a blurred overlay of equal-sized blobs with no shadows

- **Severity:** blocker (photo: blocker; artifact: major, parts 1 to 4 of its "blurred overlay" finding)
- **Screenshots:** `C:\dev\fable-space-game\out\screens\atmosphere\leo-day.png` (popcorn x 640-1000, y 420-600; flat patches x 660-960, y 560-720). Same folder: `leo-day-limb.png`, `sampling-low-orbit-limb.png`, `sampling-low-orbit-limb-animated.png`, `clouds-quality-0.7-orbit.png`, `perf-low-orbit-1440p.png` (x 1300-1940, y 800-1160 native).
- **Defect:** From 400 km the cloud layer is a blurred field of same-sized round blobs (bubble wrap: 10 to 15 px, about 7 to 10 km each at 720p) and soft white smears. No cloud casts a shadow on the sea at a sun elevation of 50 deg, although the brief asks for decks that cast shadows. No cloud has a lit and a shaded side or any relief. Large clouds are flat, with posterised two-tone patches and straight-sided brighter plateaus. The brightness is one flat light grey. There is no thin cirrus, no cloud street, and no texture below the blob size. Everything in the layer is softer than the planet's own edge, so it reads as a low-resolution procedural overlay. The native 1440p animated frame shows the same blobs, only larger.
- **Checked:** Confirmed. `leo-day.png`: equal-sized blobs across the frame, no shadow on the sea anywhere, the large cloud at (820, 650) is (186, 183, 181), and the brightest pixel in the frame is 203. A 2x crop of x 640-1000 shows the bubble-wrap cells, and a crop of the large cloud shows the two-tone patches with straight edges. A 2x native crop of `perf-low-orbit-1440p.png` shows the same blobs and no shadows. The stipple toward the limb is item 7.
- **Real reference:** ISS day-side photographs at the same obliquity: clouds are the sharpest thing in the frame, with detail to the pixel; popcorn cumulus of every size down to sub-pixel sparkle, organised in streets and open or closed cells; each cloud with a crisp dark shadow displaced on the sea; towers and anvils with bright sunward flanks, grey lee sides and long shadows on the decks below; translucent cirrus veils; cloud brightness from brilliant white to thin grey.
- **Likely cause:**
  - Pattern: at orbit footprints (0.4 to 0.7 km) the shape noise is past its useful mips (`cloudNoiseKeep`, l.57, fades between levels 4 and 5.5; the level here is about 4.4 of a 9 km tile). What is seen is `cLow` modulated by one octave of inverted Worley cells of the mesoscale noise (`nMeso = meso.g`; `mesoscaleScaleM` 40 km / 4 cells = 10 km) in `cloudColumnAt` (l.110-125): round cells of a single scale.
  - Shading: each pixel is a cloud fraction lit with the mean light of a column (`cloudStackStep`), so there is nothing to shade. Opacity saturates: `lowCoverageGain` 0.9 on a map of which about a quarter is at 0.9 to 1 makes `col.cLow` 1 over whole systems, and the extinction variation (`lowDensityVariation`) fades out with `mesoKeep` and `largeKeep`.
  - Shadows: the near shadow map's half extent grows with altitude (clouds.ts l.692: 30 km + 2.5 x altitude, about 1,000 km at 400 km, so about 1.9 km per texel, band-limited further by `shadowFilter`). That is coarser than the 1 to 4 km shadow offsets. Beyond the map, the far term is a constant optical depth of 2 times the mip-filtered coverage.
  - Resolution: half-resolution march over a quarter of the area, bilinear upsample, cloud geometry read by a nearest `textureLoad` (shaders/march.ts l.186), and the coverage map never read at its finest level (`max(log2(footprint / texel), 1.0)`).
- **Fix direction:** Give the orbit regime its own detail and its own shadow.
  - Detail: a multi-octave, anisotropic mesoscale field (streets along the wind, cells from 50 km down to the pixel), or tiling cloud-texture sets per cloud type derived from photographs (the terrain package already builds materials from photo sources), driving fraction and optical depth. Bake the variation into a mip-consistent texture so it survives at every distance.
  - Relief: a cloud-top height from that field, shaded by the sun through its gradient where shapes are unresolved.
  - Shadows: per pixel, evaluate the same column density at the point displaced along the sun direction by the cloud-top height (a parallax shadow on the surface and on lower cloud, at the pixel's footprint) instead of a 2 km shadow map.
  - Resolution: march this regime at full resolution (it needs few steps per ray), or upsample edge-aware with the geometry filtered like the colour. Read the coverage map at its finest level from orbit.
  - Compare with ISS frames at 1440p.

### 3. At low sun the cloud-shadow map shows through as saw teeth, straight stripes and combs

- **Severity:** blocker (photo: blocker, artifact: major)
- **Screenshots:** `C:\dev\fable-space-game\out\screens\atmosphere\clouds-ground-sunset.png` (teeth x 420-840, y 340-440; ground stripes x 700-1280, y 465-545). Same folder: `clouds-ground-cumulus-into-sun.png` (x 420-700, y 170-260), `leo-low-sun.png` (x 380-620, y 600-720), `perf-low-orbit-terminator-1440p.png` (x 900-1300, y 1180-1440 native, animated).
- **Defect:** Whenever the view looks toward a low sun, the near shadow map is visible in the cloud lighting and in the light shafts.
  1. Sunset from the ground (sun 3 deg up): the clouds in front of the sun are cut by a row of dark zig-zag triangles and wedge-shaped shafts fanning out from the sun (teeth 20 to 40 px wide, visible without zooming), and the ground carries knife-straight light and shadow stripes.
  2. From orbit with the sun 4 deg up: a comb of 1 to 2 px streaks parallel to the sun's azimuth hangs from the edge of every cloud, across cloud and sea. It is in the native 1440p animated frame too, so temporal accumulation does not hide it.
  3. Sun 40 deg up, looking into it from the ground: the cloud in front of the sun is shaded in axis-aligned staircase facets with straight treads 15 to 30 px long, and the silver lining is a beaded outline 1 to 2 px wide.
- **Checked:** Confirmed in all four frames. A 3x crop of the sunset frame shows the row of dark triangles, and a 2x crop of the ground shows the ruler-straight wedges. A 4x crop of `leo-low-sun.png` shows the comb running from the cloud edge down over the sea, and a 3x native crop of `perf-low-orbit-terminator-1440p.png` shows the same comb in animated mode. The facets in `clouds-ground-cumulus-into-sun.png` are visible at 4x and are the subtlest of the three.
- **Real reference:** Sunset under broken cumulus: soft crepuscular rays whose edges are penumbrae that widen with distance (the sun is half a degree wide, which is 9 m of penumbra per kilometre), clouds shading each other in smooth gradients, silver linings that are a graded glow, and lit ground patches with edges hundreds of metres soft. From orbit at low sun: long, soft-edged, tapering shadows of cloud towers on decks and sea, never parallel hairlines or saw teeth.
- **Likely cause:**
  - The map (`placeShadow` in clouds.ts) is 1024^2 over 60 km near the ground (59 m texels) and is projected along the sun line with the elevation clamped to `SHADOW_MIN_ELEVATION` = 4 deg (clouds.ts l.51 and l.717). At sunset one texel is stretched about 14 times along the sun azimuth, and below 4 deg the map's direction is not the sun's at all.
  - `cloudShadowFsWgsl` (shaders/clouds.ts l.913-984) marches the whole 11.3 km layer along the projection line in `shadowSteps` = 16 equal steps per texel. At the clamp that line is about 160 km long and one step about 10 km, so `sFront` (channel g, where the cloud begins) is quantised to 10 km and neighbouring texels land on different quanta.
  - The lookups in `atmoCloudShadowAt` (shaders/march.ts l.25-39) and in the core's `cloudShadow` (`src\render\core\shaders\chunksLighting.ts`) take one bilinear tap and combine three unlike channels after interpolation, `tau = clamp((g - s) * b, 0, r)`. A front distance interpolated across a cloud edge is a ramp, which gives the teeth, the facets and the streaks along the sun direction.
  - The cloud march adds the map beyond the 2,500 m cone (`tau += -log(atmoCloudShadowAt(endPos ...))`), so texel blocks appear inside the cloud.
  - In deterministic mode the shadow lookup in the air march has no dither (`misc.z` = 0), which freezes the comb. The comb is in the animated frame as well.
- **Fix direction:** Either make the map adequate at low sun or stop using it there.
  - Map: a step count proportional to the path length through the occupied altitude range, stratified and jittered per texel. Store filterable quantities (transmittance at a few fixed depths, which is a small deep or opacity shadow map, or exponential or moment optical depth) instead of front distance plus mean extinction, and pre-filter it. Until then, resolve the three channels per texel and interpolate the resulting transmittance (four taps); never interpolate g across a cloud edge.
  - Penumbra: widen the filter with distance from the occluder (PCSS style, with the sun's angular radius).
  - Low sun (under about 8 deg): a warped or cascaded map along the sun azimuth instead of the 4 deg clamp, or a blend to ray-marched samples along the true sun direction (a longer cone of 8 to 12 blue-noise samples, accumulated) for clouds and shafts. For the ground use the coverage term with a penumbra that grows with distance.
  - Dither the lookup with blue noise in the cloud march as well, and keep a spatial dither in deterministic mode.
  - Add a sunset close-up to the specs with a bound on high-frequency energy along the sun direction. Re-shoot the sunset, into-sun and low-sun orbit close-ups at 1440p.

## Major

### 4. Whatever a cloud shades turns navy and far too dark

- **Severity:** major (photo: major, artifact: major)
- **Screenshots:** `C:\dev\fable-space-game\out\screens\atmosphere\clouds-under-deck.png`. Same folder: `clouds-shadow-under-deck.png`, `shadow-lit-quad-under-the-deck.png`, `clouds-ground-cumulus.png` (mountains y 440-490; ground band y 495-540), `clouds-ground-cumulus-into-sun.png`, `perf-ground-cumulus-overhead-1440p.png`, `ascent-10km.png` (x 560-760, y 540-640), `sampling-ascent-5km-horizon-animated.png` (shadows on the islands).
- **Defect:** Everything in cloud shadow is saturated navy and much too dark: a blue dusk under a white ceiling. Under the overcast deck the deck is neutral grey while the sea, the islands and the haze over the far islands are blue. The lit test surface under the deck is at 0.002 of its clear-sky value (hand-off section 5, not checked), and the daylight-exposure frame under the deck is nearly black. From the pad, cumulus shadows on the mountains and the plain are flat navy patches with crisp outlines, about 6.7 times darker than the sunlit slope beside them with the hue flipped from beige to navy, and the shadowed ground is a blue-black band. From 5 to 10 km, cloud shadows on the pink islands have the colour of open sea and read as lakes cut into the land.
- **Checked:** Confirmed, with these measurements. `clouds-under-deck.png`: deck (117, 114, 113), sea (17, 26, 43), island (55, 66, 87), haze over the far islands (122, 131, 149). `clouds-shadow-under-deck.png`: frame mean (6, 10, 21), brightest value 64. `clouds-ground-cumulus.png`: mountain in shadow (41, 50, 69), in sun (137, 128, 123); ground band (11, 17, 27). `ascent-10km.png`: shadow on land at (673, 583) = (79, 99, 132), open sea at (680, 673) = (80, 96, 125). `shadow-lit-quad-under-the-deck.png`: a near-black quad on a navy field.
- **Real reference:** Under a thick overcast the ground receives 10 to 25 % of the clear-sky illuminance as white diffuse light: a grey scene with no blue cast, a grey sea reflecting the cloud base, land in its own colours, grey-white haze. Shadows of cumulus on a partly cloudy day are 3 to 5 times darker than sunlit ground and only slightly cooler, because the sky and the bright clouds around fill them.
- **Likely cause:** Nothing the package publishes knows about clouds except the direct-sun shadow. It publishes an optical depth (`clouds.shadowMap`, `farShadowOpticalDepth`) and the clear-sky `atmosphere.skyIrradianceLut`. The deferred lighting (`src\render\core\lighting\deferred.ts` l.206 `sunE *= cloudShadow(...)`, l.229-260 ambient from the clear-sky table and water reflecting `atmosphere.skyViewLut`, which has no clouds; chunk `cloud_shadow` in `src\render\core\shaders\chunksLighting.ts`) applies pure Beer-Lambert exp(-tau / muS) to the sun and adds the unshadowed blue clear-sky irradiance. The light that a cloud transmits diffusely (two-stream: roughly 1 / (1 + 0.1 tau) of the sun under a deck) and the light of the cloud base and sides are missing. In `marchFsWgsl` (shaders/march.ts l.234) only `o.direct` is multiplied by `atmoCloudShadowOnRay` while `o.multi` stays the clear-sky multiple scattering, so the air under a deck glows blue. The clouds themselves are lit with the two-stream term (`cloudMarchFsWgsl` l.722-731), so cloud and ground disagree.
- **Fix direction:**
  - Carry a diffuse term with the shadow: E_ground = E_sun x mu x [exp(-tau / mu) + (T_diffuse(tau) - exp(-tau / mu))], the second part white, with T_diffuse from the same two-stream constants as the cloud lighting.
  - Replace the sky irradiance under cloud by a cover-weighted mix, (1 - f) x E_clear_sky + f x E_cloud_base (grey-white), with f from the coverage map and the shadow map.
  - The core's `cloud_shadow` chunk has to return (direct, diffuse). File the request to core-support with the formula and the map channel or second texture that carries it (or fold it into what `setCloudShadowParams` hands to the core).
  - Apply the same factor to `o.multi` below the cloud base in the march and in the aerial volume, and add the cloud layer to the sky-view table (or publish a cloud-aware variant) so that water under an overcast reflects grey.
  - Extend `shadows.spec.ts`: under the deck, 0.1 to 0.3 of the clear value and a neutral hue (B/R within 25 % of the sunlit one) for ground, sea and haze; a cumulus shadow on terrain keeps 20 to 35 % of the lit luminance.

### 5. Overcast decks never look like cloud: contour lines, dunes, terraces and veins

- **Severity:** major (photo: major, artifact: major)
- **Screenshots:** `C:\dev\fable-space-game\out\screens\atmosphere\tunables-shipped.png` (for example x 380-780, y 330-590). Same folder: the ten other `tunables-*.png`, `clouds-deck-top.png`, `clouds-above-deck.png`, `perf-above-a-deck-1440p.png`, `shadow-lit-quad-above-the-deck.png` (x 60-430, y 280-450), `shadow-lit-quad-low-orbit.png`, `transparent-quad-over-clouds-from-orbit.png`, `transparent-quad-under-clouds-from-orbit.png`, `transparent-quad-under-deck.png` (x 850-1000, y 560-640), `clouds-under-deck.png` (x 700-1200, y 180-360).
- **Defect:** An overcast does not look like cloud from any side.
  1. From 25 km with the shipped tunables the whole deck is a vermiculated pattern of nested wavy iso-lines, like wood grain or a topographic map.
  2. From 6 km straight down, the smooth part of the deck is drawn as flat terraces with outlines and meets the textured part along a hard boundary.
  3. From just above (4 to 6 km) at a shallow angle the deck is smooth snow dunes with dark smudged pits, hairline creases and a ruler-straight horizon.
  4. In animated mode the lines turn into a sandpaper stipple (see also item 14).
  5. Straight down from 400 km an overcast is mid-grey mottled marble with bright worm-like veins along the noise cell edges. In the transparent orbit test it is a blank, perfectly uniform grey card.
  6. From below, the base is a watercolour smear with thin bright vein lines and one hard-edged swoosh.
- **Checked:** Confirmed. `tunables-shipped.png` is covered with iso-lines edge to edge. Centre crops of nine other tunables frames show the same lines in most of them; they are strongest in `tunables-no-erosion-hard-edges.png` and faint in `tunables-strong-erosion-soft-edges.png` and `tunables-thin-extinction.png`. A 3x crop of `shadow-lit-quad-above-the-deck.png` shows the terraces with their outlines. `shadow-lit-quad-low-orbit.png` is grey marble with bright veins, mean (139, 136, 134). `transparent-quad-over-clouds-from-orbit.png` is uniform at (199, 195, 191) with a standard deviation of 1.5. `clouds-above-deck.png` shows the dunes, creases and straight horizon; `clouds-under-deck.png` shows the veins and the swoosh; a crop of `transparent-quad-under-deck.png` shows stepped contours.
- **Real reference:** Stratocumulus from an airliner, a U-2 or the ISS: a quilt of closed cells or cotton-ball lumps 0.5 to 2 km across with bright rounded centres and darker, thinner edges, pebbly texture everywhere with soft continuous shading, long shadows at low sun, bright white (albedo 0.5 to 0.7) and never mid-grey, the horizon softened by haze. From below: soft mottling of darker and lighter grey. No lines from any side.
- **Likely cause:** The march loop in shaders/clouds.ts.
  - Dunes: sheet tops are an analytic bell profile with `topReliefM` 700 m of the 9 km shape noise carved in (`cloudDensityAt` l.244-255), which has only low frequencies.
  - Contour lines: slabs restart at the bisected cloud surface (l.618-644) and each slab is lit once at its opacity-weighted depth; grazing hits are lit as one "sliver" slab; slabs that add little reuse the light of the last lit slab (`reuseBelow`, l.501 and l.693-697). The number of slabs a ray spends in the bright skin changes in whole steps across the surface, and with the step phase frozen (`xi` = 0.5) each step is an iso-line. The source comments themselves name "contour bands, sand" as the failure of slab placement.
  - Terrace boundary: the switch `shaped = (low && den.resolved > 0.5) || den.relief > 1.0` is a threshold, not a blend.
  - Veins: thin gaps between `col.lowTop` and `col.highBase` (`cloudColumnAt` l.138), the `(nr - 0.5) * 0.35 * (1 - fade)` term at the fade boundary, and the Worley-cell channel (`nb.a`, `meso.g`) entering density and relief with creases.
  - Grey from orbit: the unresolved deck is lit by the `highOrders` term at the mean optical depth, and nothing ties its brightness to the reflectance of a thick layer.
- **Fix direction:**
  - Remove the quantisation instead of dithering it: light every slab in the first few mean free paths (no reuse there), interpolate the light between consecutive lit slabs, place slab boundaries by accumulated optical depth instead of by distance, and dither slab starts with blue noise.
  - Blend the shaped and smooth representations over a range of `den.resolved` and relief instead of switching.
  - Give decks a cell structure of their own: a Worley-based closed-cell height field (cells 0.7 to 2 km, several octaves, domain-warped, on top of a smooth fBm) for the top, opacity dips at the cell edges, and a relief of 150 to 400 m that both the eye march and the light march resolve.
  - Make deck brightness follow the two-stream reflectance, tau / (tau + about 8), so that thick decks are white from above. Where cells are unresolved, shade by the gradient of the cell field.
  - Add assertions: no line-like features in `tunables-shipped` and on an overcast from 6 and 25 km in both modes (a ridge detector, gradient-direction coherence, or Laplacian zero-crossing density), and a deck reflectance range from 25 km and from 400 km.

### 6. Looking down during ascent (5 to 80 km) the cumulus are flat white splats

The altitude rule that would settle it (l.287-291) covers only a camera above the shell top or below its bottom (`h0 > cloudShell.w`, `h0 < cloudShell.z`); a camera between 1.2 and 12.5 km, which is the whole ascent through the troposphere, falls through to the distance rule. Why the frozen step phase produces radiance without geometry there is not established; the resolve's `o.color.a > 0.9995 || geom.b > 1.0e37 -> NO_CLOUD` branch keeps the colour while dropping the geometry.
- **Fix direction:** Make the two candidates agree by construction: when the nearest surface of the block lies below the cloud base (`hNear < cloudShell.z`) and the cloud begins nearer than the surface, every cloud on the ray is in front of it, wherever the camera is and whatever the geometry texel says. Sample cloud radiance and geometry with the same filter. Never let the resolve write colour with alpha below 1 next to `NO_CLOUD` geometry (zero the colour, or keep the last geometry). Then find why the frozen step phase produces the veil at all. Extend `sampling.spec.ts`: run the row-period test (periods 2 to 8) on full-resolution rows over the whole frame below the horizon, in both modes, and compare the frozen and the accumulated frame there.
