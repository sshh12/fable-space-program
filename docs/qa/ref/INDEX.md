# Reference photograph library

Real photographs for side-by-side comparison with renders. Every image was opened and checked by eye: all are real photographs or camera frames, not illustrations or renders. All are resized to at most 1600 px on the long side (JPEG q90). Sources: the NASA Image and Video Library (ISS / Shuttle / Apollo / KSC / Artemis crew and agency photographs, public domain) and Wikimedia Commons (licence per file; CC BY / CC BY-SA files need attribution to the named author if republished. Internal comparison use is fine). Some older ISS frames carry a white frame-ID strip along the bottom edge; ignore it when measuring. Two subjects share one photo: earth-orbit-ocean/glint-from-aircraft-hawaii is a copy of sky-ground-clouds/cu-hawaii-from-aircraft.

**How the numbers were measured.** `Y` is Rec.709 luminance of the sRGB-decoded pixel values (0-1), and `rgb` is the mean or median 8-bit sRGB of a region. They are relative to each photo's own exposure and tone curve, so treat ratios as camera-referred rough values. Clipped highlights make bright-to-dark ratios LOWER bounds. Use them to check a render's ordering and approximate contrast (for example 'cloud tops 13-16x the open ocean at high sun'), not as absolute radiance.

## earth-orbit-clouds

Cloud fields, terminator, limb and airglow from low Earth orbit (ISS / Shuttle crew photographs).
Key numbers: at high sun cumulus tops are **13-16x** the open-ocean luminance (ocean Y 0.04-0.07, rgb ~(25-45, 60-75, 85-100)); cloud flanks in shade stay blue-grey (46,56,75), never black.
At low sun, tower shadows on the haze/cloud layer below are only **2.3-3.3x** darker than the sunlit haze and are blue-grey (65,82,94) against warm lit haze (154,138,120). Oblique views add a bright blue haze: background Y rises to 0.15-0.25 and cloud contrast drops to ~3x.

## earth-orbit-ocean

Sun glint, slicks, internal waves, sea colour and shallow water from orbit, plus one airliner view of a glitter path.
Key numbers: deep ocean off-glint is dark navy, Y 0.04-0.11, rgb (7-55, 60-95, 85-143), and almost textureless at ISS scale (quirimbas p10-p95 spread only +-5%).
In orbital photos the glint core is **7.5-9x** the off-glint sea and warm white (209,184,145)-(232,220,203); from an aircraft the glitter path clips and is **>40x** the sea beside it. Shallow sand banks and lagoons are **3-3.5x** brighter than deep water and turquoise (113,168,168).

## sky-ground-clouds

Fair-weather cumulus, stratocumulus, overcast and sunset skies from the ground and from aircraft (Wikimedia Commons).
Key numbers: zenith-side sky Y 0.06-0.09, rgb ~(38,78,140), brightening **4-5x** and desaturating to (154,168,180) toward the horizon.
Thin cumulus tops are **4-6x** the zenith-side sky, and their bases are flat, level, grey-blue (150-165,161-178,174-195) and STILL ~2.5x brighter than the sky. Only thick stratocumulus has bases darker than the sky (0.6x). Overcast is nearly uniform (p95/p5 1.8x), bluish grey (98,104,114). From 10-11 km the sky overhead darkens to (26,53,85) and the horizon band is near-white (189,192,196), a 15x range.

## sea-surface-close

Open-ocean waves and whitecaps from a few metres to a few hundred metres, and capsule splashdowns (Apollo 9/17, Orion EFT-1, Artemis II, Dragon).
Key numbers: whitecaps at Beaufort 6-7 cover only **~5-10%** of the near-field sea, mostly less, as streaks with bright cores and translucent tails.
The sea mirrors the sky. Under overcast it is ~0.2x the sky (85,95,104). Under blue sky, sea just below the horizon is ~0.45x the sky and sea looked down on is 0.13-0.15x (Fresnel). Calm open ocean seen from above is deep navy (27-40, 62-77, 95-116).

## liftoff-ascent

Lift-off by day and night (Saturn V, Shuttle, SLS Artemis I/II, Atlas V), engine ignition, plume at altitude, twilight 'jellyfish' plumes and a stage separation.

## spacecraft-hardware
