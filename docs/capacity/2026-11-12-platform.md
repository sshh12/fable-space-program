# Capacity and cost report: platform-delivery, stage 0 skeleton (2026-11-12)

First measured cloud numbers of the v2 deployment (ARCHITECTURE 10.4, 16.2, 29.2, 30; hosting-small section 9).
Everything below was measured on the deployed stage 0 on Railway's Hobby plan (project `fable-space-program`,
US East, Metal region us-east4-eqdc4a) or from this machine against it, unless it says "local stack". The
server roles are the hello stand-in of this wave (no universe yet): every idle figure here is a floor for the
real `game`, not its measurement.

**First lines.**

- **The bucket FAILS at 2,000 concurrent fetchers**: 2.74 % errors (pass at or under 0.5 %), p95 first byte
  553 ms (pass at or under 400 ms), 1.40 Gbit/s sustained (pass at or above 1.5). It passes at 20 and at 500.
  Stage 0 is unaffected (20 fetchers is its scale). The escalation of OPEN_QUESTIONS ("Outside the three
  providers") is the lead's to raise before any launch is announced; the mirror at launch size (the other half
  of that trigger) is measured in the launch rehearsal. Section 6 has the caveats of this run.
- **The boot set does not hold levels 0-3 of Earth and the Moon**: those alone are 122 MB on disk; the boot
  pack holds what fits the 25 MB budget by priority and lists the rest as deferred (section 9).
- **The first session needs about twice the bytes the cost model assumed**: 577 MB to orbit on the bucket path
  in the browser as it is today (sky 135 MB and assets 136 MB are fetched whole), 300 MB in ARCHITECTURE 30.
  With the OPFS cache the unique tile bytes of that session are 190 MB (section 8).
- **Cost ceiling: met** on every reading so far (U + $5 = $8.75 against $30 on a 25-minute window that
  includes the measurement traffic; section 1). The 6-hour and 72-hour idle readings are not taken yet: they
  need the skeleton to stand untouched for that long after this session (runbook in section 1).

## 1. Cost: the one formula (ARCHITECTURE 29.2)

`U` = the game project's usage at list prices from Railway's per-project, per-service figures
(`railway usage projects --project fable-space-program --json`), over a window, projected to 730 hours. The
ceiling is judged on `U + $5` (as if the game paid the whole plan fee) against about $30 idle and $50 at 5
players; the workspace's bill, `max($5, U + the other project's projection)`, is beside it. Readings are
appended to `docs/capacity/railway-usage.jsonl` by `node deploy/railway/usage.mjs snapshot` and the window is
computed by `node deploy/railway/usage.mjs report --from=<iso> --to=<iso>`.

  +6 h and +72 h; `node deploy/railway/usage.mjs report --from=<first> --to=<second>` for each window. The

## 6. Bucket throughput from a Modal fleet (D6)

Probe `deploy/modal/fsp_probe.py` (ephemeral app `fsp-probe`, environment `fsp`), 15 minutes per level, real
tile ranges (21,665: tile groups drawn from the Moon's index in proportion to every level and face, plus the
recorded first session's tile reads; mean 26 kB), presigned URLs of the deployed pack list, 50 fetchers per
container. Pass: p95 first byte of a cold range <= 400 ms, errors <= 0.5 %, >= 1.5 Gbit/s sustained at 2,000.

| Fetchers | Containers | Requests | Errors | First byte p50 / p95 / p99 | Bytes | Sustained | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 20 | 1 | 71,126 | 0 | 223 / 378 / 733 ms | 1.86 GB | 0.017 Gbit/s | **pass** |
| 500 | 10 | 4,688,661 | 0.018 % (714 truncated bodies, 111 timeouts) | 48 / 233 / 388 ms | 123.0 GB | 1.07 Gbit/s | **pass** |
| 2,000 | 40 | 6,897,002 | **2.74 %** (174,605 truncated bodies, 14,451 timeouts) | 173 / **553** / 728 ms | 162.5 GB | **1.40 Gbit/s** | **FAIL** on all three |

Raw report: `docs/capacity/bucket-probe-2026-11-12.json`. Caveats, said plainly and not as excuses: the request
list drew on the Moon's packs (3.39 GB; the Earth's were still uploading when it was built), so the working set
was smaller than a launch day's (if anything a kinder test for the storage's caches); the fleet's own capacity
was 40 containers of 1 CPU each with 50 fetchers per container, and the errors are aiohttp's
`ClientPayloadError` (a body cut short) and timeouts (30 s), which a saturated client could also produce; the
first-byte time at 20 fetchers is a single container's (its p95 is higher than at 500, where connections are
warm in ten containers). Modal's placement of the containers was not pinned (no region selection on the
plan). A rerun with 80 containers of 25 fetchers, and with the Earth's packs in the list, separates client
limits from bucket limits: `npx tsx deploy/modal/make-requests.ts && modal run --env fsp deploy/modal/fsp_probe.py
--levels 2000 --per-container 25` (80 containers needs the workspace's container limit of 100 to be free).
Cost of the three levels on Modal: about 765 container-minutes of 1 CPU and 1 GB, about $1 at list price,
inside the monthly credit; nothing on Railway (bucket egress is free).

## 7. The cloud derating (D7)

`deploy/bench/derating.ts` (job role `derating`): six regimes of the real mission as whole `createSim` world steps
with real terrain (the pad ascent with the first stage burning, low Earth orbit coasting, Earth entry with
heating, the descent under drogues, the lunar powered descent at 60 % throttle, landed on the Moon); each worker
thread holds one sim of each and steps all six on every 120 Hz tick; 1, 2, 4 and 8 workers step the same tick
(a shared counter advanced by the main thread, which sleeps to each boundary; the workers block on it; a late
tick is caught up, never skipped); 30 s per level after one simulated second of warm-up. Railway: a temporary
replica at 8 vCPU / 8 GB (the `ops` service with ROLES=derating, deleted after the run; the same hardware class
as `game`, whose own limit stayed at 2 / 2), terrain through the pack source from the packs bucket. This
machine: the same bundle, terrain from data/tiles, with other agents' work running (rule 16: provisional).

Per-thread time per tick (six world steps), microseconds, p50 / p99 (ticks over the 8.33 ms budget of 3,600):

| Workers | This machine (Ryzen 9 9950X3D) | Railway (Intel Xeon Gold 5520+, 8 vCPU limit) | Derating p50 / p99 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 204 / 2,394 (0) | 667 / 6,786 (14) | 3.3x / 2.8x |
| 2 | 177 / 2,557 | 665 / 7,938 (26, 36) | 3.8x / 3.1x |
| 4 | 248 / 3,072 | 557 / 6,132 (14-17 per thread) | 2.2x / 2.0x |
| 8 | 397 / 4,319 | 625 / 7,126 (13-30 per thread) | 1.6x / 1.6x |

| | This machine | Railway |
|---|---|---|
| vCPU-seconds per vessel-second (1 / 2 / 4 / 8 workers) | 0.0100 / 0.0109 / 0.0111 / 0.0155 | 0.0274 / 0.0255 / 0.0216 / 0.0229 |
| Idle CPU of the process (no worker, 20 s) | 0.0040 vCPU | 0.00004 vCPU |

Reading: one Railway thread steps these six regimes at about 100-110 us per world step at the median (this
machine 30-35 us), so the derating is about 3x at one or two workers and falls to 1.6-2.2x at four and eight,
where this machine's own threads contend (other agents ran beside it) while the Railway replica's eight threads
do not slow each other. The p99 per tick is 6-8 ms on Railway: 0.4-1 % of ticks pass the 8.33 ms budget with six
world steps in them, which says the conservative "about 2.5x" of ARCHITECTURE 30 holds and that the real-time
lane's 50 % utilisation target (29.2) is about 40 world steps of these regimes per thread at the median, fewer
at the tail. 16 and 22 workers need a Pro replica (the launch rehearsal). Logs: `out/platform/derating-local.log`,
`out/platform/derating-railway-logs.txt` (not tracked).

Without any client cache (every repeated read fetched again): 64 KiB gap 6,137 requests (4.2x fewer than per
file), 128 KiB 4,728 (5.5x).

**Recomputed figures** (ARCHITECTURE 10.4 and 30 assumed 300 MB of tiles and data per new visitor):

- Per new visitor as the browser loads today: 304 MB of tiles + 135 MB of sky + 136 MB of assets + 2.5 MB of
  code and definitions = **577 MB** on the bucket path (1.9 x 300 MB). With the OPFS cache in front of the
  tiles: 190 MB of tiles, 461 MB in all. Sky and assets are loaded whole today; the boot pack carries their
  low mips and the rest could stream on demand (capability-settings, app-integration-1).
- Through the mirror at 256 KiB slices: 1.38 bytes per byte needed, so a visitor that falls back to the
  mirror costs 461 MB x 1.38 / 1 GB x $0.05 = **$0.032** (ARCHITECTURE said $0.014) if every request missed the
  CDN.
- The front-page day: 50,000 visitors x 461-577 MB = **23-29 TB** (ARCHITECTURE: 15 TB); if every byte came
  through the mirror and missed: 23 TB x 1.38 x $0.05/GB = **$1,590-1,990** (ARCHITECTURE: $750-1,125).
- The mirror's daily budget (8 GB, $0.40 a day) now covers about 12 first visitors a day that fall back to it
  (ARCHITECTURE: about 26).
- At stage 0 (36 new visitors a day, 1,080 a month) on the bucket path the tile bytes stay free; 5-player
  months are unchanged.

## 9. The boot set (local stack, `--page=build`)

Boot pack 5b7c3d4fe9256522a06c637853572595: 19.97 MB, plus the client build's first paint 2.22 MB (index.html
and the entry chunk) and the definitions 0.23 MB = 22.4 MB of the 25 MB budget. Included, by priority:
manifests, the star catalogue, Earth albedo, water mask, land cover and night lights levels 0-2, Earth height
0-1, the Milky Way's mips 3-11 on all six faces, the low-resolution albedo maps, Moon albedo 0-2 and height
0-1, Earth water mask, land cover and night lights level 3. Deferred: Earth height 2 (9.4 MB) and 3 (35.5 MB),
Earth albedo 3 (10.6 MB), Moon height 2 (9.9 MB) and 3 (37.2 MB), Moon albedo 3 (3.4 MB); no low-resolution
cloud map is baked; there is no menu yet. Cold load over 50 Mbit/s (local stack, `--page=build`, real Chrome,
clean context, cache disabled, 20 ms latency): **22.56 MB in 4.05 s** against the 8 s budget (local stack: the
deployed cold start is deploy-loadtest's to measure).
