Build a AAA-quality, photorealistic spaceflight simulator that runs in desktop Chrome/Edge on WebGPU, on a modern dedicated GPU. Don't build a WebGL fallback or a mobile version.

World
- A photorealistic Earth at true scale (atmosphere, clouds, oceans, day/night), with a fictional but plausible launch pad to start from.
- The real Moon at its true size and distance, with terrain detailed enough to fly over and land on.
- Every body must look photorealistic at every distance: standing on the pad, climbing through the atmosphere, in orbit, and far out in deep space. The view must change seamlessly during flight, with no loading screens, visible LOD popping, or swaps between separate scenes.
- KSP-style physics: patched conics with spheres of influence, on rails while coasting and integrated during burns and in atmosphere, plus atmospheric drag, reentry heating, and time warp. Keep the true scale: solve the precision and LOD problems it creates instead of shrinking the world.

Vehicle
- A fictional but plausible modern multi-stage rocket with a crew capsule and a lunar lander. It needs convincing engine plumes and burns, visible stage separation, and full sound design (ignition, rumble, staging, reentry, muffled in vacuum).

Controls & UI
- KSP-style controls: a navball, throttle, pitch/yaw/roll, a stage stack that the player fires by hand, and SAS with hold modes (stability, prograde/retrograde, normal/antinormal, radial in/out). The player flies every burn by hand: no autopilots.
- Three views: a first-person cockpit (no EVA for now), a third-person chase cam, and a map view for planning orbits (trajectories, Ap/Pe, maneuver nodes).

Architecture
- Don't hard-code this one mission. Planets, moons, vehicles, and launch sites should be data-driven, so we can add new ones later without rewriting the engine. Leave room to add EVA later, both floating in space and walking on planet and moon surfaces.

Don't cut corners
- Rendering: physically based atmospheric scattering (sky color, sunsets, the glow at the planet's edge from orbit), real elevation data with streamed high-res terrain on Earth and the Moon, Sun lighting, and a real starfield. No blue gradients, halo sprites, or smooth spheres.
- Simulation: Earth rotates (launches pick up its spin), the Moon really orbits, and landings collide with the actual terrain. The map view draws true predicted trajectories across SOI changes, with maneuver nodes the player can edit. The navball is a true 3D ball with prograde/retrograde, normal, and radial markers.
- Vehicle: detailed models and a real cockpit interior. Plumes respond to throttle and air pressure, and spent stages fall away as physical objects. Reentry shows plasma, builds heat, and can destroy the ship. Splashdown needs parachutes and a real ocean. Sound reacts to throttle, altitude, and camera position.
- Finish: no placeholder art, stubs, or TODOs, and 60 fps on the target GPU. To call it done, fly the whole mission under real physics. Test scripts may drive the controls, but no teleporting or cheats.

Process
- Work on your own until you're done, and only stop if you're truly blocked. Check your own work as you go with automated tests and browser screenshots.
- When you use workflows or subagents, be mindful of model choice and scale: match the model to each task, and don't start more agents than the work needs.

Resources
- The .env file has keys for OpenAI, Hugging Face, and ElevenLabs. The Modal CLI is logged in for GPU jobs; leave the existing Modal apps alone. Use any of these if they help (textures, sound effects, GPU jobs), up to $500 per provider. Never commit the keys or ship them to the client.

These requirements extend the original scope; exceptions below apply only where stated.

World & vehicles
- Expand to the entire solar system at true scale, with all planets and major moons on real orbits. Keep the Earth and Moon detail above; other bodies may use placeholder visuals and simple surfaces for now, overriding the original terrain and no-placeholder requirements for those bodies only. Players must be able to reach and orbit them. Design for landing in a future update.
- Offer a varied fleet of plausible rockets and spacecraft for different capabilities and flight profiles. Players freely choose a vehicle and launch from Earth; don't tie vehicles to prescribed missions.

Live multiplayer
- Design and implement the backend, networking, and hosting. Choose Modal, Netlify, Railway, or a combination to fit the architecture; credentials are already set up. Create a GitHub repo called fable-space-program for the project.
- Build around lobbies, but put everyone in one shared mega-lobby for now. Architect for front-page-of-Hacker-News traffic, with basic load tests and documented capacity, bottlenecks, and a scaling path.
- Players share a live universe and can rendezvous, dock with each other, undock, and crash into each other freely. Support multiple Earth launch pads and concurrent launches. Decide how docked craft share control.
- Persist ships and docked assemblies across logout, reconnects, and server restarts. Choose sensible, configurable universe-timeout and cleanup rules for abandoned ships and debris; define the clock used and how activity and docking affect expiry, and make the rules visible to players.
- Choose and implement the most fun workable multiplayer time-warp model. Resolve how players at different flight stages coexist, synchronize for encounters, and handle collisions, docking, and persistent ships through warp.
- Extend the KSP-style map with live player craft, predicted intercepts, closest approaches, relative motion, and maneuver planning for rendezvous across spheres of influence.

Presentation & access
- Give the front end AAA polish: a Battlefield 1-inspired menu over a live Earth and space view, with vehicle selection and a seamless transition into spawning and launch.
- Detect WebGPU/device capabilities and run a short performance benchmark to automatically choose graphics settings; allow manual adjustment. Clearly block mobile and unsupported devices with an explanation. Keep the desktop WebGPU target.

Don't cut corners
- Shared simulation: use server-authoritative state and a coherent time model. Handle latency, reconnects, and simultaneous interactions; nearby players must agree on positions, burns, collisions, and damage. Don't fake multiplayer with cosmetic ghost ships or client-only outcomes.
- Docking and collisions: implement relative-velocity contact, collision response, docking-port alignment, persistent physical assemblies, and undocking with consistent momentum and control. No overlapping ships disguised as docking. Keep fast-moving craft from tunneling through each other, and handle launch-pad contention.
- Time warp: implement the synchronization rules end to end, including entering and leaving warp near other craft, burns, SOI transitions, logout, and cleanup. No divergent timelines silently merged by teleporting ships, or a global warp control that makes everyone else's flight unplayable.
- Map and scale: derive trajectories and intercepts from the actual simulation and its time model, including moving targets and SOI changes. Preserve precision for close docking anywhere in the solar system. No decorative orbit lines, scripted rendezvous, or shrinking distances.
- Persistence and capacity: save authoritative state durably and recover it after a restart, including docked assemblies. Scale simulation and network updates by proximity and relevance while keeping the mega-lobby coherent; don't broadcast every object every frame. Bound abandoned-object growth and measure load with simulated active players, not just idle connections or HTTP requests.
- Verification: test with separate clients flying, docking, colliding, warping, disconnecting, and reconnecting, including under latency. Demonstrate restart recovery, expiry behavior, an interplanetary flight into orbit, and concurrent launches. Report measured load-test limits honestly; architecture alone isn't proof of capacity.
